HIGH COURT OF CALDERA
Northfield Residents Association v. Republic of Caldera
This document is invented. It exists only to try the TRACE code.
The claimants are an association of residents of the coastal town of Northfield. They ask the Court to declare that
the Republic has not done enough to reduce its emissions and to order it to do more.
The Republic answers that its emissions are a tiny part of the world total and that any order would interfere with
the choices of Parliament.
1

<!-- page 2 -->

II. ASSESSMENT
The Court first considers the argument that the emissions of the Republic are too small to matter.
In Example Foundation v. State of Exempla the Supreme Court of Exempla held that a state cannot escape its
responsibility by pointing to the small size of its own contribution to a global harm.
This Court sees no reason to depart from that reasoning, which applies with equal force to the Republic.
Every state is one of many contributors, and the harm would never be addressed if each of them could hide behind
the others.
2

<!-- page 3 -->

The Court then turns to the content of the obligation of the Republic.
The duty of care owed by the Republic to its residents requires it to take reasonable and timely measures against a
known and serious risk.
That duty is not discharged by plans that remain on paper, nor by targets that are announced and then postponed.
The residents of Northfield have shown that the risk to their homes from the rising sea is both known and serious.
3

<!-- page 4 -->

III. CONCLUSION
The Court has found that the Republic is responsible for its share and owes a duty of care to its residents.
A state that contributes to a global harm answers for its own part of it, and the courts may hold it to a minimum
reduction target.2
The choice of the measures that achieve the target remains with Parliament.
The claim is therefore allowed in part.
4
2 Example Foundation v. State of Exempla, EX:SC:2019:88, para. 5.3.
