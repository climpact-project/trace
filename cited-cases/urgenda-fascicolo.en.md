## 0. What it is for and how to read it

You are given a passage from ANOTHER case. You must say whether it takes up a proposition from Urgenda (RIPRESA), whether it asserts something similar based on another source (ALTRA_FONTE), or whether it does not assert it (NON_AFFERMA). Three things to always keep in mind. Links in the chain: (a) serious risk, known to science, deferred, affecting the population; (b) duty of the State to act; (c) the small share does not exonerate; (d) minimum from scientific consensus, determinable by the judge; (e) order as to result, freedom as to means.

1. Urgenda is not only a civil-law case. It has two tracks, and both belong to it. The first instance (2015) bases the obligation on the unwritten duty of care of Art. 6:162 BW, using the ECHR only as a source of interpretation. The appeal (2018) and the Supreme Court (2019) base it on the positive obligations of Arts. 2 and 8 ECHR and no longer examine the civil-law route (Hof § 76). A text that bases the State's climate obligation on Arts. 2 and 8 ECHR is on the Urgenda 2018-2019 track, not on another path.
2. Many sources arrive on their own at similar conclusions: Neubauer, KlimaSeniorinnen, the ICJ, ITLOS and Inter-American opinions, the duty of care in Shell. Section 4 states for each one what it asserts, on what basis, with which words one recognises it, and whether it in turn cites Urgenda.
3. Not naming Urgenda does not exclude a ripresa. What counts is the argumentative path and the formulas; the name is only the strongest clue.

## 1. The three rulings

### 1.1 The Hague District Court, 24 June 2015 (first instance)

`decision_f1ad`, 55 pp.; reasoning in section 4 (pp. 30-53, paras. 4.1-4.110), facts in 2.1-2.78.

Reasoning. It establishes as fact the IPCC consensus (4.11-4.31): beyond 2 degrees the danger is serious and irreversible; for the 450 ppm scenario, Annex I must reduce by 25-40% by 2020 compared to 1990; the Netherlands aims at approximately 17%. «[I]t is currently very probable that within several decades dangerous climate change will occur» (4.32, p. 36). It then seeks a duty of the State towards Urgenda (4.35-4.93) and finds it by exclusion: Article 21 of the Constitution leaves broad discretion (4.36); UNFCCC, Kyoto and no-harm bind only between States (4.42) but act as «reflex effect» on open domestic norms (4.43), as does EU law (4.44); Urgenda is not a victim under Article 34 ECHR and does not directly invoke Articles 2 and 8 (4.45), which, however, are «a source of interpretation» of the unwritten duty of care of Article 6:162 BW (4.46). The duty is constructed with the Kelderluik judgment on hazardous negligence (4.54) and with the UNFCCC and TFEU principles, equity, precaution, sustainability, prevention (4.55-4.63), applied in six factors (4.63-4.82); the State controls the level of national emissions (4.66). The defence of negligible contribution (0.5% global, 4.78) fails because every emission contributes, with the analogy of the Kalimijnen judgment (4.79). Conclusion: «the State has a duty of care to take mitigation measures» (4.83); below 25% it acts negligently and unlawfully; more is not imposed out of respect for discretion (4.86, 4.93). The separation of powers is not an obstacle (4.94-4.102): the State «will retain full freedom ... to determine how to comply» (4.101). Operative part: at least -25% by the end of 2020 (pp. 53-54).

Basis. Civil law: Article 6:162 BW, Kelderluik, Kalimijnen. ECHR only as a source of interpretation (4.46); international law and EU law by indirect effect. CBDR becomes «principle of fairness» and the idea that industrialised countries «have to take the lead» (4.57). Paris is not there; it is subsequent.

### 1.2 Gerechtshof Den Haag, 9 October 2018 (appeal)

`decision_8a5e`, 19 pp., paras. 1-76 («legal ground» in the translation).

Reasoning. Article 34 ECHR concerns only access to Strasbourg: with Article 3:305a BW Urgenda invokes Articles 2 and 8, with direct effect, for residents (35-36); the current generation suffices, «in particular but not limited to the younger individuals» (37). From Articles 2 and 8 arises the positive obligation to prevent future violations, «(in short: a duty of care)» (41, with Öneryıldız, Budayeva, Kolyadenko, Fadeyeva); no impossible burden, «wide margin of appreciation» on the means (42); in the face of a «real and imminent» known threat, precautionary measures are required (43). From the facts (44) a real threat to the current generation, and the duty to protect (45). The 25-40% of AR4, referred to by the COPs without direct effect (48-51); the State itself aimed at 30% without ever justifying the reduction (52); 25% is «in line with the State's duty of care» (53). Defences rejected: ETS and waterbed, each Member State has a «individual responsibility» (54-56); carbon leakage (57); adaptation (59); group target (60); minimal emissions and global problem (61-62); uncertainty, with precaution and Tătar (63); causal link (64); separation of powers (67); order to legislate (68); trias politica (69). The State violates «its duty of care pursuant to Articles 2 and 8 ECHR»; 25% is a minimum (73); the margin applies to the means, not to the minimum (74); the civil-law route need not be examined (76).

Basis. Articles 2 and 8 ECHR as positive obligations, in the hybrid lexicon «duty of care» (41, 73, 76). UNFCCC, IPCC and COP as evidence of consensus; precaution (63); Paris as confirmation of 1.5 (15, 50).

### 1.3 Hoge Raad, 20 December 2019 (cassation)

`judgment_ed17`, 47 pp. It opens with a «Summary of the Decision» (pp. 2-7) written by the court, which states that it is not substituting the grounds: it is the text most echoed abroad. Grounds in sections 4-8, three-level numbering.

Path. Section 4: uncontested premises; every emission consumes the carbon budget (4.6); the threat established by the Hof (4.7). Section 5: Article 2 imposes measures against a «real and immediate risk» known to the State, where «immediate» means that the risk directly threatens, not that it is imminent in time (5.2.2); Article 8 for serious environmental damage, even if not short-term (5.2.3); protection of the entire population (5.3.1); preventive obligation consistent with precaution, no margin of appreciation on the an, discretion on the means (5.3.2); judicial review and due diligence, with Jugheli (5.3.3); no impossible burden, obligation of measures and not of guaranteed result (5.3.4); effective interpretation and «common ground» (5.4); effective remedy under Article 13 (5.5). That the risk materializes in decades and affects large parts of the population does not exclude protection (5.6.2). Then the global issue: the Netherlands must make «its part» (5.7.1) because UNFCCC and Paris presuppose that all act (5.7.2-5.7.4); no-harm justifies the «partial responsibility» (5.7.5), in line with Article 47 ARSIWA and national civil liability (5.7.6); the small-share defense and the defense of others' inaction are rejected (5.7.7), because «no reduction is negligible» (5.7.8); rights must be invocable against the individual State (5.7.9). Conclusion: Articles 2 and 8 oblige States «to do 'their part'» (5.8), according to their share (5.9.1); Urgenda acts under 3:305a (5.9.2-5.9.3). Section 6: the apportionment is in principle political (6.2), but the judge verifies the lower limit and the State cannot do nothing (6.3); with a clear international consensus based on science, it establishes the «minimum fair share» (6.4); it reviews due diligence and good governance and the burden of reasoning (6.5); only «in clear-cut cases» (6.6). Section 7: genealogy of the 25-40% (7.2.1-7.2.4), not superseded by AR5 (7.2.5), reinforced by 1.5 and precaution (7.2.8-7.2.10), «common ground» (7.2.11), valid for the Netherlands individually (7.3.2, 7.3.6); deferral policy not reasoned (7.4); the 25% is «an absolute minimum» (7.5.1). Section 8: if obliged, the State can be ordered to comply (Article 3:296 BW, 8.2.1); only ordering legislation is prohibited «with a particular content» (8.2.4); the declaratory judgment and the order of measures for an objective are allowed (8.2.6); the 25% order leaves the means free (8.2.7); the judge reviews the limits of law, including the ECHR (8.3.2-8.3.3); exceptional case (8.3.4).

Basis. Only Articles 2 and 8 ECHR (with 1 and 13), applied under Articles 93-94 of the Constitution and interpreted as the ECtHR (environmental case law: see 4.15). One's own share rests on UNFCCC and Paris, no-harm, Article 47 ARSIWA, Kalimijnen and PETL 3:105 (note 35), Massachusetts v. EPA (note 36). The minimum on IPCC AR4, COP, EU and «common ground» (Demir and Baykara, Nada). The civil-law path appears only in the summaries of the lower instances (2.2.2, 2.3.1).

Two practical consequences. A text predating 9 October 2018 cannot adopt the Urgenda ECHR route nor the formulas of the Supreme Court: if it adopts Urgenda, it adopts the first instance. A text that writes «duty of care» together with Arts. 2 and 8 ECHR is using the Hof formula.

### 2.1 How to recognise the degree from the number

- Three-level number (5.7.7, 8.2.6), or two-level from 5.2 to 8.4 (5.8, 6.4, 7.1): Hoge Raad. 5.1 is ambiguous: in the Hoge Raad it is the State's thesis, in the Rechtbank it is the operative part (as cited by OC-32/25, note 394). The same applies to references with «legal ground 5.6.2» (as Shell 2021 writes them) or «r.o.»/«rov.» (Dutch «rechtsoverweging»).
- Whole number from 1 to 76 («para. 45», «§ 64», «no. 64», «legal ground 45»): Gerechtshof 2018.
- 4.9-4.110: Rechtbank 2015. Caution: 4.1-4.8 exist in both the Rechtbank (introduction and standing) and the Hoge Raad (premises on danger). «Hoge Raad ... 4.6» is the carbon budget; «Rechtbank 4.6» is standing under 3:305a. Also 2.x exists in both (facts).
- Three-digit numbers after the point (2.79, 4.222, 4.237): conclusie of the Procurator General (Langemeijer and Wissink, 13/9/2019, ECLI:NL:PHR:2019:1026), not a court. Whoever cites it cites the Urgenda file, but the entry is «conclusioni».
- ECLI: RBDHA:2015:7145/7196 = Rechtbank; GHDHA:2018:2591/2610 = Hof; HR:2019:2006/2007 = Hoge Raad; PHR:2019:887/1026 = conclusie. Case numbers: C/09/456689 / HA ZA 13-1396 (Rechtbank), 200.178.245/01 (Hof), 19/00135 (Hoge Raad).
- Recurring errors in citing texts, verified in the corpus: OC-32/25 dates the Rechtbank «24 de junio de 2014» (note 394); an amicus in Juliana writes «ECLI:NL:HR:2029:2006»; an opposition in Juliana attributes the role 19/00135 to the «Hague Ct. App.»; the Grande-Synthe press release dates the Hof «8 November 2018». A wrong reference in the date remains a reference to Urgenda.

### 2.2 Three corrections with respect to `urgenda.yaml`

1. The canonical passage of `ordine_non_legge` («They may also order the public body in question to take measures in order to achieve a certain goal, as long as that order does not amount to an order to create legislation with a particular content») is para. 8.2.6, not 8.2.7. Section 8.2.7 is the application to the case («leaves the State free to choose the measures»). Verified against the Dutch text (`judgment_4966`, pp. 35-36). Those who cite «para. 8.2.7» for the abstract proposition are off by one paragraph but cite Urgenda.
2. The passage on due diligence and Jugheli is 5.3.3; section 5.3.4 concerns the impossible or disproportionate burden.
3. «[C]an serve as a source of interpretation» of the Rechtbank is 4.46; 4.45 is the denial of victim status (as confirmed by the judgment itself at 4.109).

## 4. The world around: competing sources

For each: what it states, on what basis, how it is recognised, whether it cites Urgenda.

### 4.1 Neubauer et al. v. Germany (BVerfG, 24 March 2021, 1 BvR 2656/18)

`neubauer-et-al-v-germany-order_7977` (English), `..._order_c68f` (German). Basis: Art. 2(2) GG (protection of life and health) and Art. 20a GG (climate protection). The duty of care is NOT violated: the review is limited to «manifestly unsuitable» or «completely inadequate» measures (§§ 152-157). The violation lies in future freedom: the emissions permitted until 2030 consume the «remaining budget» and shift the burdens onto the period thereafter; fundamental rights operate «as intertemporal guarantees of freedom» (Headnote 4, p. 2). § 149 (p. 43): «The fact that the German state is incapable of halting climate change on its own ... does not, in principle, rule out the possibility of a duty of protection arising from fundamental rights». Headnote 2c (p. 2): «The state cannot evade its responsibility by pointing to greenhouse gas emissions in other states.» Remedy: order to the legislature to legislate by 31 December 2022, the opposite of an order as to the result.

It is recognized by: Art. 20a GG, intertemporal freedom, remaining budget, «offloaded onto the future» burdens, Klimaschutzgesetz, Sachverstaendigenrat.

It cites Urgenda: yes, as «Hoge Raad of the Netherlands» (or Rechtbank, Gerechtshof), never by name and never in § 149. § 202 (p. 60, «The state may not evade its responsibility here by pointing to greenhouse gas emissions in other states») cites Gerechtshof no. 64 and Hoge Raad no. 5.7.7; § 199 (p. 59) Rechtbank 4.90; § 157 (p. 45) Rechtbank 4.75 and HR 7.5.2; § 161 (p. 46) HR 4.2 and 4.4; § 121 (p. 38) HR 7.4.3; only in the German § 218 (p. 92) HR 2.1, 4.6, 7.4.3. Headnote 2c and § 202 therefore rest on Urgenda (see 5.4).

### 4.2 Verein KlimaSeniorinnen Schweiz v. Switzerland (ECtHR, GC, 9/4/2024, no. 53600/20)

`verein-klimaseniorinnen-schweiz-and-others-v-switzerland-judgment_f4b1`. It decides under Art. 8 (Art. 2 «unnecessary», §§ 536-537): right to effective protection against the serious effects of climate change (§ 544); «the State’s obligation under Article 8 is to do its part to ensure such protection» (§ 545, p. 206). Its own share from CBDR (Art. 3 § 1 UNFCCC, Paris, Glasgow): «each State has its own share of responsibilities to take measures to tackle climate change» (§ 442, p. 177); concurrent responsibility and Art. 47 ARSIWA, commentary §§ 6 and 8 (§ 443). On the «“drop in the ocean” argument»: no «but for», it suffices that the omitted measures «could have had a real prospect of altering the outcome or mitigating the harm» (§ 444, p. 178). Reduced margin on objectives, wide margin on means (§ 543); five requirements including a «overall remaining carbon budget» (§ 550); on the budget it follows Neubauer (§ 571: «The Court also finds convincing the reasoning of the GFCC»). Individual victim status only with high-intensity exposure and «pressing need» (§§ 487-488); associations under three conditions (§ 502). No order: execution left to the Committee of Ministers (§ 657).

It is recognized by: «do its part» together with the reduced/wide margin, «overall remaining carbon budget», «real prospect of altering the outcome», «pressing need», 53600/20.

It cites Urgenda: never in the Court's assessment. Only in the descriptive parts: § 164 (p. 73, report of the UN Special Rapporteur), §§ 245-246 (p. 113, French materials), §§ 260-261 (pp. 118-119, comparative law), § 263 (pp. 123-124, the Norwegian Supreme Court distinguishing Urgenda). «Do its part» coincides with the HR without referral (see 5.3).

### 4.3 Duarte Agostinho v. Portugal and 32 States (ECtHR, GC, 9/4/2024, inadmissibility)

`duarte-agostinho-and-others-v-portugal-and-32-other-states-decision_554b`. No merits. Jurisdiction only of Portugal; «no grounds in the Convention for the extension, by way of judicial interpretation, of the respondent States' extraterritorial jurisdiction» (§ 213, p. 78); failure to exhaust domestic remedies (§§ 226-228). One's own share serves here to DENY jurisdiction: § 202 (p. 75) takes up KlimaSeniorinnen § 442: «There is therefore no risk of a vacuum in the protection of Convention rights». Urgenda appears only in the observations of the Dutch Government (§ 106, p. 43: «as was illustrated by the judgment of the Supreme Court of the Netherlands in Urgenda[nota 20], practical and effective judicial protection against the consequences of climate change was already possible under the Convention»).

### 4.4 Greenpeace Nordic

(a) Norwegian Supreme Court, plenary, 22/12/2020 (People v Arctic Oil), `greenpeace-nordic-assn-v-ministry-of-petroleum-and-energy-people-v-arctic-oil-judgment_2f86`. Art. 112 of the Norwegian Constitution: «Article 112 of the Constitution must, when the Storting has considered a case, be interpreted as a safety valve.» (§ 142, p. 25); annulment only if the legislature has «grossly neglected» its duties. Territorial logic: «the clear principle is that each state is responsible for combustion on its own territory.» (§ 159, p. 28). Arts. 2 and 8 ECHR: risk not «real and immediate», no direct link (§§ 166-171). Cites Urgenda to DISTINGUISH it: «The judgment from the Netherlands has little transfer value to the case at hand.» (§ 173, p. 30), because it concerned general objectives and not the validity of an act.

(b) ECtHR, Greenpeace Nordic v. Norway, 28/10/2025, no. 34068/21, `greenpeace-nordic-and-others-v-norway-judgment_86b9`. No violation; only procedural obligations. It takes up from KlimaSeniorinnen «do its part» (§ 314) and the margin on means (§ 315). Core: «an adequate, timely and comprehensive environmental impact assessment in good faith and based on the best available science must be conducted before authorising a potentially dangerous activity» (§ 318, p. 87), with combustion emissions «both within the country and abroad» (§ 319). It does not cite Urgenda.

### 4.5 Leghari v. Pakistan (Lahore High Court, 4 and 14/9/2015; 25/1/2018)

`leghari-v-federation-of-pakistan-decision_0d83`, `..._judgment_5be2`. Arts. 9 (life) and 14 (dignity) of the Pakistani Constitution; delay in implementing climate policy, especially adaptation: «the delay and lethargy of the State in implementing the Framework offends the fundamental rights of the citizens» (0d83, p. 6, § 8); «we need to move to Climate Change Justice» (§ 7). Remedial measure (focal person, Climate Change Commission, then Standing Committee). No quota: the polluter «by and large falls outside the national jurisdiction» (5be2, p. 22, § 21). Recognized by «Climate Change Justice», «Water Justice», «delay and lethargy». It does not cite Urgenda.

### 4.6 Juliana v. United States (D. Or., Aiken, 10/11/2016; Ninth Circuit, 17/1/2020)

Aiken, under the due process clause: «I have no doubt that the right to a climate system capable of sustaining human life is fundamental to a free and ordered society.» (`juliana-v-united-states_522f`, `oregon-federal-court-said-young-people-could-pursue-constitu...`, p. 32), plus the «danger creation» (duty arising from having created the danger). On causation, he argues from the LARGE share of the USA, not from «no reduction is negligible». Ninth Circuit (Hurwitz): «Reluctantly, we conclude that such relief is beyond our constitutional power.» (`divided-ninth-circuit-said-juliana-plaintiffs-lacked-standin...`, p. 11): it is the polarity of `ordine_non_legge` and `target_giudiziale`. Staton dissent: every reduction counts, against those who reduce the remedy to «just a drop in the bucket» (pp. 45-46), on a US constitutional basis. It is recognized from «capable of sustaining human life», «danger creation», «redressability», «Article III». It cites Urgenda: neither Aiken nor the Ninth Circuit; it is cited by the Findings of Magistrate Judge Coffin (8/4/2016, p. 11: «a Dutch court, on June 24, 2015, did order a reduction of greenhouse gas emissions nationwide by at least 25% by 2020. See Urgenda»), amici (with HR 5.7.7-5.7.8, Hof 45 and 73, Rechtbank 4.83) and briefs.

### 4.7 Sacchi v. Argentina and others (UN Committee on the Rights of the Child, 22/9/2021)

A decision per State (`decision_fede` Argentina, §§ 10.x; `decision_1009` Germany, §§ 9.x). Extraterritorial jurisdiction with the OC-23/17 test (§ 10.7, p. 11): causal link, effective control, foreseeability. «the collective nature of the causation of climate change does not absolve the State party of its individual responsibility» (§ 10.10, p. 12), but based on CBDR, Paris and Art. 47 ARSIWA (note 24) and used for jurisdiction. Inadmissible for failure to exhaust domestic remedies. It is recognized from «effective control», «reasonably foreseeable», «ratione loci». Urgenda appears only in the decision on Germany, in the applicants' arguments (§ 2.6, note 9) and in the special rapporteurs' intervention (§ 6.5, note 16).

### 4.8 Milieudefensie v. Royal Dutch Shell (The Hague District Court, 26/5/2021; Court of Appeal, 12/11/2024)

`milieudefensie-et-al-v-royal-dutch-shell-plc-judgment_4679` (2021), `..._7bfb` (2024). The most insidious false friend: same court, same Article 6:162 BW, known as «Urgenda 2.0». 2021: duty of reduction of a COMPANY arising from unwritten due diligence (4.4.1), read in light of human rights (not directly applicable to Shell) and the UN Guiding Principles on Business and Human Rights; obligation of result on Scope 1, «significant best-efforts obligation» on Scope 3; at least -45% net by 2030 compared to 2019. § 4.4.49 (p. 39) mirrors HR 5.7.7-5.7.8 and rejects the substitution argument: «this does not absolve RDS of its individual partial responsibility to do its part regarding the emissions of the Shell group», but in a footnote refers to Kalimijnen. 2024: confirms the duty of care (7.55), sets aside the quantified order for lack of consensus on a percentage for a company; Scope 3 not proven (7.106). It is recognized from «RDS», «Scope 1, 2 and 3», «UNGP», «individual partial responsibility», private addressee. Cites Urgenda (always the HR): 2021 § 4.4.10 (p. 28, «legal ground 5.6.2»: «From the Urgenda ruling it can be deduced that Articles 2 and 8 ECHR offer protection against the consequences of dangerous climate change»); 2024 §§ 7.7-7.8 (p. 20) and § 7.106 (p. 40: «also in line with what the Supreme Court considered in the Urgenda judgment (legal ground 5.7.7 and 5.7.8)»). Shell's figures reconstructed from the layout, except 4.4.49.

### 4.9 Commune de Grande-Synthe v. France (Conseil d'Etat 19/11/2020 and 1/7/2021)

`municipality-of-grande-synthe-v-france-decision_7eca` (2020), `..._decision_cb9c` (2021), `..._opinion_fd32` (public rapporteur). The court reviews the compatibility of the government's refusal with the TRAJECTORY that the State has set for itself by law (-40% by 2030, art. L. 100-4 code de l'energie, carbon budget): «le refus opposé à la requérante par le pouvoir réglementaire est incompatible avec la trajectoire de réduction de ces émissions fixée par le décret du 21 avril 2020» (2021, § 6, p. 6); injunction to take «toutes mesures utiles». `controllo_di_un_target_legislativo` is excluded. The decisions do not cite Urgenda; the conclusions of Hoynck (§ 2.2, pp. 6-8) set it out and dismiss it: «I am not suggesting that you should use this solution based on the ECHR».

### 4.10 Inter-American Court, Advisory Opinion OC-23/17 (15/11/2017)

`...-opinion_029c` (Spanish). American Convention and San Salvador Protocol. Healthy environment as «derecho autónomo» (§ 62); jurisdiction of the State of origin «si existe una relación de causalidad entre el hecho que se originó en su territorio y la afectación de los derechos humanos de personas fuera de su territorio» (§ 101, p. 44); prevention with due diligence «proporcional al grado de riesgo» (§ 142); precaution (§ 180). No quota, target, or order. Extraterritoriality comes from here (or from Sacchi), not from Urgenda. It does not cite Urgenda.

### 4.11 ITLOS, advisory opinion 21/5/2024 (COSIS, Case No. 31)

`...-international-law-opinion_4f32`. UNCLOS, Part XII. Emissions constitute marine pollution (§ 179); «The standard of due diligence under article 194, paragraph 1, of the Convention is stringent» (§ 243, p. 88), with implementation according to capabilities (§§ 241, 243); precaution is part of due diligence (§ 242); obligation of conduct: «It is thus the conduct of a State, not the result which would be entailed by the conduct, that will determine whether the State has complied» (§ 233, p. 84); Paris is not sufficient (§§ 223-224). It is recognized by «pollution of the marine environment», «article 194». It does not cite Urgenda.

### 4.12 ICJ, Obligations of States in respect of Climate Change (23/7/2025, General List No. 187)

`...-climate-change-decision_af16` (page markers do not coincide with the printed pages). Climate treaties, custom (prevention, cooperation), UNCLOS, human rights, State responsibility. «the standard of due diligence for preventing significant harm to the climate system is stringent» (§ 138, p. 57); 1.5 as «primary temperature goal» (§ 224); NDCs with the «highest possible ambition» (§§ 240-246); no-harm from Trail Smelter to Pulp Mills (§ 272); «it is scientifically possible to determine each State’s total contribution to global emissions» (§ 429, p. 129); plurality of responsible parties (§§ 430-431). Close to Urgenda on one's own share, but interstate and with its own sources. It is recognized by «erga omnes», «highest possible ambition», «plurality of injured or responsible States». It does not cite Urgenda (only «national courts», §§ 385, 391).

### 4.13 Inter-American Court, Advisory Opinion OC-32/25 (29/5/2025)

### 4.15 Non-jurisprudential sources and ECtHR case law

Paris Agreement. In Urgenda: Hof §§ 15 and 50 (confirmation of 1.5); HR 5.7.4, 7.2.8 (art. 2(1)(a)), 7.3.2 (individual responsibility). Urgenda does not use «highest possible ambition», «progression» nor the NDCs as a criterion. Signals: these = Paris read on its own (ICJ, ITLOS, KlimaSeniorinnen §§ 546-547); «25-40%», «Annex I», «by 2020» = Urgenda.

UNFCCC and CBDR. In Urgenda: Rechtbank 2.35-2.38 and 4.55-4.59 (equity, precaution, sustainability); HR 5.7.2-5.7.3 to conclude that each does «its part», 6.2 to say that the apportionment is political. Rule: CBDR used to DIFFERENTIATE between States («historical responsibility», capacity) = UNFCCC read on its own (ICJ § 148; excluded `responsabilita_comuni_differenziate`); used to DENY the excuse of the small share, with «its part» or «partial responsibility» = Urgenda. KlimaSeniorinnen § 442 makes the passage from CBDR to one's own share without citing Urgenda.

ARSIWA art. 47. In Urgenda only HR 5.7.6 (commentary §§ 6 and 8, the river polluted by several States). The same §§ in KlimaSeniorinnen § 443, and in Sacchi note 24. Alone it does not decide; with «partial responsibility», «partial fault», «its part» it signals Urgenda.

No-harm. Rechtbank 4.39 («no state has the right to use its territory, or have it used, to cause significant damage to other states»), 4.42, 4.52; HR 5.7.5 as the basis of partial responsibility. Urgenda does not name Trail Smelter, Corfu Channel, Pulp Mills, Stockholm 21, Rio 2. «Transboundary», «significant harm», «Principle 21», «all the means at its disposal» = custom (ICJ § 272, ITLOS § 246; excluded `no_harm_transfrontaliero`); no-harm bent into a duty towards one's OWN residents via the ECHR = Urgenda.

Precaution. Rechtbank 4.58, 4.60-4.61, 4.76; Hof 43, 63, 73; HR 5.3.2, 5.6.2, 5.7.3, 7.2.5, 7.2.10, 7.4.6. Signals of Urgenda: «High plausibility, as described above, suffices» (Hof 63), «as safe as possible» (Hof 73), «more far-reaching measures ... rather than less far-reaching measures» (HR 7.2.10), precaution within the ECHR positive obligations. From another source: «precautionary approach», «Principle 15» (ICJ § 158), «integral part» of due diligence (ITLOS § 242).

ECtHR environmental case law. The Rechtbank does not name judgments (Council of Europe Manual, 4.47-4.50); Hof § 41 Öneryıldız, Budayeva, Kolyadenko, Fadeyeva and § 63 Tătar § 120; HR in note: 5.2.2 Öneryıldız, Budayeva, Kolyadenko; 5.2.3 Taşkın, Tătar; 5.3.1 Di Sarno, Cordella § 172; 5.3.2 Tătar § 120; 5.3.3 Fadeyeva, Budayeva, Cordella §§ 161-174, Dubetska, Jugheli (transcribed § 76). Osman and López Ostra do not appear. KlimaSeniorinnen reconstructs the same matrix from its own case law (§§ 507-518, 538). Rule: the judgment on a landfill, a foundry, a landslide is the matrix (excluded `inquinamento_industriale_locale`, `obblighi_positivi_generici`); the PASSAGE from there to a global, deferred and diffuse risk, with one's own share, is Urgenda's move (HR 5.6.2) and since 2024 also KlimaSeniorinnen's. Lexicons: HR «real and immediate risk» extended to the long term; Hof «real and imminent threat»; KlimaSeniorinnen «serious, genuine and sufficiently ascertainable threat ... material and temporal proximity» (§ 513, p. 196).

## 5. How to recognise a resumption

### 5.1 Strong indicators
- Name, ECLI, docket number, or a form of `urgenda.yaml` (even OCR: «Lirgenda», «Uruenda»); «Hoge Raad of the Netherlands», «Dutch Supreme Court», «District Court of The Hague» in a climate context.
- A paragraph number from section 2 without a name («supra note 33, paras. 5.7.5-5.7.7»): decipher it with 2.1.
- The formulas from section 3 or their faithful translations: «its part/their part» of the State towards its residents, «partial responsibility», «no reduction is negligible», «minimum fair share», «absolute minimum», «real and immediate risk» extended to a risk spanning decades, «order to create legislation with a particular content».
- Two or more rings in the Urgenda order (deferred risk, obligation towards residents, own share, minimum from consensus, freedom of means), even with other sources alongside.
- «Duty of care» + Articles 2 and 8 ECHR (the Hof formula); the State's civil-law duty of care for insufficient mitigation (the Rechtbank formula).

### 5.2 False friends
Neubauer (intertemporal freedom, Art. 20a GG, remaining budget); KlimaSeniorinnen (CBDR § 442, «real prospect» § 444, requirements § 550); ICJ, ITLOS, OC-32/25 opinions (due diligence «stringent» or «reforzada», NDC, erga omnes, healthy climate, marine pollution); OC-23/17 and Sacchi (extraterritoriality); Shell (duty of care of an undertaking, UNGP, Scope 1-3); Juliana (due process, danger creation, redressability); Grande-Synthe (target set by law); Leghari (life and dignity, adaptation); Massachusetts v. EPA (source, not resumption). Polarity: whoever states the rejected defense (negligible share, global risk outside the ECHR, disguised order to legislate, political quantification) does not resume the proposition; whoever rejects it with the words of Urgenda does.

### 5.3 Uncertain cases
- Urgenda formula without referral in a text subsequent to 9/4/2024 («do its part», «its own share»): it may come from KlimaSeniorinnen. Reduced/wide margin, carbon budget, «real prospect», 53600/20 lead to Strasbourg; «partial responsibility», «no reduction is negligible», 25%, «minimum fair share», Annex I lead to Urgenda. If nothing decides and the text follows the rings in Urgenda's order, in doubt the resumption prevails.
- The ECHR route is Urgenda's route since 2018. It becomes another source only if the text expressly relies on KlimaSeniorinnen or on other Strasbourg case law without making the passage to the deferred global risk with one's own share.
- Before 9/10/2018 no one can resume Urgenda's ECHR route (the Rechtbank denied it, 4.45): one can resume the Rechtbank on diligence, on the share, on the minimum, on the order.

### 5.4 Mediated references
Neubauer § 202 (HR 5.7.7, Hof 64) and § 199 (Rechtbank 4.90), Shell 2021 § 4.4.10 (HR 5.6.2), OC-32/25 note 394 (Rechtbank 4.83-4.84) build on the point in Urgenda. If the passage takes up the proposition from one of these points, the chain passes through Urgenda: indicate the intermediate source in the «fondamento» and flag the reference as nested (codebook, dimension «annidata») instead of classifying it as ALTRA_FONTE. If it takes it up from a point that does not rest on Urgenda (Neubauer on intertemporal freedom, KlimaSeniorinnen § 442 on CBDR), it is ALTRA_FONTE.

### 5.5 Who is speaking
The name may appear in the descriptive part, in a party's submissions, in an amicus brief, or in a cited report (KlimaSeniorinnen §§ 164, 245-246, 260-261; Duarte § 106; Sacchi §§ 2.6, 6.5): the court does not adopt the proposition as its own. State this in the «mossa»: who asserts it, and whether the adjudicating body adopts, summarises, or distinguishes it (Norwegian Supreme Court § 173).
