## 0. What it is for and how to read it

You are given a passage from ANOTHER document. You must state whether it takes up a proposition from the opinion (RIPRESA), whether it asserts something similar based on another source (ALTRA_FONTE), or whether it does not assert it (NON_AFFERMA). The seven propositions (probes) are in 1.4: they are only the elements PECULIAR to this opinion. The generic statements that the opinion shares with other sources (cooperation is a duty, human rights depend on the environment, adaptation) are not probes.

Five things to always keep in mind.

1. The opinion does not decide a dispute. It does not establish the responsibility of any State (§§ 106, 108, 406) and does not quantify anything. Whoever cites it to say that a specific State has violated something applies it, rather than reproducing it literally: it is still a RIPRESA if the proposition is used, but «mossa» must say so.
2. The title is also the name of the proceedings. «Obligations of States in respect of Climate Change» is written at the head of every memorial of 2024 and in resolution 77/276 of 2023. Before 23 July 2025 no one can cite the opinion: whoever uses that title beforehand names the request. Afterwards, many notes cite the documents of the proceedings («Kenya, Written Statement to the International Court of Justice in Obligations of States in respect of Climate Change (Advisory Opinion) (March 2024)»): they are not citations of the opinion.
3. Three climate opinions travel together. ITLOS (21/5/2024, COSIS), the Inter-American Court (OC-32/25, 29/5/2025) and the ICJ (23/7/2025) are almost always cited in a chain. Many sentences of the ICJ come from ITLOS and the Court says so (due diligence «stringent», national system of control, cumulative effects, cooperation). The ICJ calls ITLOS «Climate Change, Advisory Opinion, ITLOS Reports 2024»: whoever writes in this way cites ITLOS.
4. The opinion speaks at the level of general international law. Its addressees are States; individuals have rights only where a treaty provides for them (§ 111). The group rule (5.6) also counts, however, the same reasoning applied to a company or relied upon by a party.
5. The reception in the corpus, today, is concentrated. Of the documents citing the opinion with paragraph numbers, the majority are memorials in the advisory proceedings before the African Court on Human and Peoples' Rights (see section 6). The national courts that have already used it are few.

## 1. The ruling

### 1.1 Identity, structure, numbering

International Court of Justice, advisory opinion of 23 July 2025, «Obligations of States in respect of Climate Change» («Obligations des États en matière de changement climatique»), General List No. 187. Request by the General Assembly pursuant to Resolution 77/276 of 29 March 2023, co-sponsored by 132 States and adopted without a vote (§ 47). President Iwasawa. Each point of the operative clause was adopted unanimously (§ 457). The French text is authoritative; the corpus contains only the English version [French text not verified in the corpus]. Annexes, not in the corpus: separate opinions and declarations by Sebutinde, Tomka, Yusuf, Xue, Bhandari, Bhandari and Cleveland, Nolte, Charlesworth, Charlesworth Brant Cleveland and Aurescu, Cleveland, Aurescu, Tladi (list at § 457, PDF pp. 139-140) [not verified in the corpus].

The two questions (set out at § 88): (a) what obligations do States have, under international law, to protect the climate system and other parts of the environment from anthropogenic greenhouse gas emissions, for States and for present and future generations; (b) what legal consequences arise from these obligations for States that, through acts and omissions, have caused significant harm, towards States (in particular small island States) and towards the peoples and individuals of present and future generations.

Structure (PDF pp. 13-140; before that, pp. 1-12, cover, composition and table of contents): procedure (§§ 1-36); I jurisdiction and discretion (§§ 37-49); II context and science (§§ 50-87); III scope and meaning of the questions (§§ 88-111); IV question (a): A applicable law (§§ 112-173), B climate treaties (§§ 174-270), C custom (§§ 271-315), D other environmental treaties (§§ 316-335), E law of the sea and sea-level rise (§§ 336-368), F human rights (§§ 369-404); V question (b): applicable law and lex specialis (§§ 405-420), attribution, causation and erga omnes (§§ 421-443), consequences (§§ 444-455); final consideration (§ 456); operative clause (§ 457).

Numbering: the paragraphs run from 1 to 457 without gaps; I counted them sequentially in the corpus text. The operative clause is entirely in § 457, with sub-points: (3) A letters a-h on treaties, B a-b on custom, C, D, E; (4) on question (b). Anyone citing «operative para. 3(E)» or «457(3)(A)(f)» is citing the operative clause, not a paragraph of the reasoning.

### 1.2 Path

Jurisdiction and discretion. Legal questions, no decisive reason to decline; the Court «states the existing law and does not legislate» (§ 48, citing *Nuclear Weapons*).

Context and science. From Resolution 2398 of 1968 to 77/276 (§§ 50-71). The Court relies mainly on IPCC reports, which the participants recognize as the best available science (§ 74); warming of 1.1 degrees caused by humans, risks increasing with every increment, adaptation insufficient (§§ 75-87).

Scope. The relevant conduct includes all acts and omissions that harm the climate system, consumption and production (§ 94), including by non-State actors under jurisdiction or control (§ 95); global scope (§ 96); temporal issues remain for assessment in concreto (§ 97). Question (b) concerns only wrongful acts, not lawful but harmful acts (§ 105).

Applicable law. The Charter, the three climate treaties, UNCLOS, ozone, biodiversity, desertification, customary duty of prevention (§§ 131-139) and of cooperation (§§ 140-142), human rights (§§ 143-145); guiding principles *infra legem*: sustainable development, common but differentiated responsibilities and respective capabilities (CBDR-RC), equity, intergenerational equity, precaution (§§ 146-161); not the polluter-pays principle (§ 160). The climate treaties do not exclude the rest: «the principle of lex specialis does not lead to a general exclusion by the climate change treaties of other rules of international law» (§ 171).

Treaties. UNFCCC: binding obligations of conduct and of result (§§ 196-208); an adopted policy «as a mere formality» does not comply (§ 208). Kyoto in force (§ 221). Paris: «the Court considers the 1.5°C threshold to be the parties’ agreed primary temperature goal for limiting the global average temperature increase under the Paris Agreement» (§ 224); NDCs with limited discretion, progression and highest possible ambition, stringent due diligence, collectively capable of 1.5 (§§ 233-249); domestic measures as an obligation of conduct, also towards private parties (§§ 250-254); adaptation (§§ 255-259); cooperation and finance (§§ 260-267); synthesis (§§ 268-270).

Custom. Prevention applied to climate: risk from cumulative effects (§§ 274-279), due diligence as the standard (§§ 280-300): regulatory measures for «deep, rapid, and sustained reductions», regulation of public and private operators (§ 282), science (§§ 283-286), COP standards and decisions (§§ 287-289), capacity (§§ 290-292), precaution (§§ 293-294), EIA (§§ 295-298), notification and consultation (§ 299). Cooperation (§§ 301-308): «Co-operation is not a matter of choice for States but a pressing need and a legal obligation» (§ 308). Treaties and custom inform each other; full compliance with the treaties gives rise to a presumption, without exhausting it, of compliance with custom (§§ 309-315).

Other regimes. Environmental treaties (§§ 316-335). UNCLOS in the wake of ITLOS, to which the Court gives «great weight» (§ 338), stringent due diligence (§§ 343-349). Sea-level rise: no obligation to update charts and coordinates (§ 362); the disappearance of a constituent element does not necessarily entail loss of statehood (§ 363); cooperation (§§ 364-365). Human rights: «the protection of the environment is a precondition for the enjoyment of human rights» (§ 373); life, non-refoulement, health, standard of living, private and family life (§§ 377-384); right to a clean, healthy and sustainable environment essential to other rights (§ 393); territorial scope (§§ 394-402); «the full enjoyment of human rights cannot be ensured without the protection of the climate system and other parts of the environment» (§ 403).

Responsibility. Customary rules, not excluded by Articles 8, 15 and 24 of Paris (§§ 407-420). Attribution: «Failure of a State to take appropriate action to protect the climate system from GHG emissions» with fossil fuels, licenses, subsidies may be unlawful (§ 427); for private actors, the State is attributed its own failure to regulate (§ 428); determinable contributions, plurality of States (§§ 429-432). Causation: «a sufficiently direct and certain causal nexus», flexible, in concreto (§§ 433-438). Obligations *erga omnes* and *erga omnes partes* (§§ 439-443). Consequences: duty to perform (a court may order a compliant NDC, § 446), cessation (§§ 447-448), full reparation (§§ 449-455).

### 1.3 Legal Basis

Treaties interpreted in accordance with Arts. 31-33 of the Vienna Convention; COP decisions as subsequent agreements (§§ 184, 224). Custom derived from the Court's jurisprudence (Corfu Channel, Nuclear Weapons § 29, Gabcikovo, Pulp Mills §§ 101 and 197, Costa Rica v. Nicaragua) and from Trail Smelter. The 2024 ITLOS Advisory Opinion is expressly cited for due diligence (§ 138), the national system (§ 281), cumulative effects (§ 276), cooperation (§ 140), UNCLOS (§§ 337-353). Science: IPCC (§§ 74, 284). Responsibility: 2001 ILC Articles, Chorzow, Armed Activities (Reparations 2022). Human rights: Human Rights Committee (Teitiota, Billy v. Australia, General Comment No. 36), Resolution 76/300, regional courts (KlimaSeniorinnen, OC-32/25, La Oroya, § 385). No national court is named: § 385 only states «and national courts». Urgenda, Neubauer, Leghari, Juliana do not appear in the text (searched in the corpus).

### 1.4 The seven probes

Second version, indicated by the PI on 01/10/2026; intervals verified against the text and disjoint. Texts, `elemento_proprio` and canonical in `cig-parere-clima.yaml`; map in `cig-parere-clima-mappa-paragrafi.yaml`.

1. `obiettivo_1_5` (§§ 223-225, 270). For the subsequent decisions of the parties, read as subsequent agreements on the interpretation of the treaty, 1.5 degrees is the agreed primary goal of Paris. Specifically: the legal characterization, not the scientific or national threshold. Canonical § 224.
2. `ndc_discrezionalita_limitata` (§§ 236-249). The discretion over NDCs is limited; the highest possible ambition is a legal standard; NDCs must together be capable of achieving the goal; formal submission is not enough. Canonical § 245.
3. `diligenza_stringente_clima` (§§ 134-139, 273-300). The customary duty to prevent significant harm applies to the climate system despite cumulative and diffuse damage, with stringent due diligence. Specifically: the general customary rule applied to climate, not the ITLOS stringent due diligence for the marine environment. Canonical § 138.
4. `non_lex_specialis` (§§ 165-171, 310-315, 335, 354, 404). Climate treaties do not exclude the rest of international law; compliance with the treaties gives rise to a presumption, without exhausting, compliance with custom. Canonical § 171.
5. `fossili_illecito` (§§ 427-438). Production, consumption, licenses and subsidies for fossil fuels, and the failure to regulate private actors, may constitute an internationally wrongful act of the State; the wrongful act is not the emission itself; attribution and causation in the case of many actors. Canonical § 427.
6. `erga_omnes` (§§ 410-420, 439-455). Obligations erga omnes and erga omnes partes; any State may invoke responsibility; loss and damage and the implementation committee do not exclude the general rules; cessation, assurances, reparation. Canonical § 440.
7. `continuita_stato` (§§ 358-363). Statehood is not necessarily lost with the loss of territory; established maritime zones are not to be redrawn. No citation in the corpus to date. Canonical § 363, bis § 362.

### 1.5 Boundary points

- Probes 1 and 2: 1.5 also appears in §§ 242 and 245 (NDCs). If the passage concerns the content of the NDCs and their ambition, it is 2; if it qualifies 1.5 as a treaty objective, it is 1.
- Probes 2 and 3: due diligence «stringent» also appears for the NDCs (§ 246) and for domestic measures (§ 254). If the passage concerns NDCs, it is 2; if it concerns the customary duty to prevent harm to the climate system, it is 3. § 254 (domestic measures) carries no probe.
- Probes 3 and 5: the regulation of private actors appears at § 282 (measures required) and at § 428 (attribution). If the passage states that the State must regulate, it is 3; if it states that the State is liable for failing to regulate, it is 5.
- Probes 4 and 6: §§ 165-171 state that the climate treaties do not exclude the other primary obligations (probe 4); §§ 410-420 state that they do not exclude the rules on responsibility and its consequences (probe 6).
- Probes 5 and 6: §§ 427-438 concern when the wrongful act occurs and who is responsible for it; §§ 439-455 concern who may invoke it and what follows from it.
- No probe, because generic or shared with other sources: cooperation (§§ 140-142, 214-218, 260-267, 301-308, 364-365), human rights and the environment (§§ 143-145, 369-403), adaptation (§§ 209-213, 255-259), CBDR-RC (§§ 148-151, 179, 226), intergenerational equity (§§ 155-157), UNCLOS (§§ 336-353), domestic measures (§§ 250-254), science (§§ 72-87), operative part (§ 457, with the correspondences noted in the map).

### 2.1 How to read a reference

- «para.», «paras», «paragraph», «¶», «¶¶», «§», «at [438]» (Australian style), «Parágrafo», «párr.», «par.»: these are paragraphs of the Court, from 1 to 457.
- «p. 73» or «at 73» are pages: of the printed edition (PDF minus 7) or of another edition [the pagination of the ICJ Reports is not verified in the corpus].
- «operative para. 3(E)», «§IV(A)(7)(d)», «457(3)(B)(a)»: the first and the third indicate the operative part (§ 457); the second a section of the opinion (IV.A.7.d = intergenerational equity, §§ 155-157).
- «(n 12) para 284», «supra note 50, ¶ 382», «Id. ¶ 73»: references to a previous footnote naming the opinion; conta_paragrafi.py reads the first two, not «Id.» and «Ibid.».
- «Separate Opinion of Judge Charlesworth, para 4», «Separate Opinion of Judges Bhandari and Cleveland, para 27»: paragraphs of a separate opinion, not of the opinion.

## 4. The world around: competing sources

### 4.1 ITLOS, Advisory Opinion of 21/5/2024 (COSIS), file `itlos-2024.yaml`
Prior case law. UNCLOS Part XII: emissions constitute marine pollution, due diligence «stringent», national enforcement system, cumulative effects, cooperation as a fundamental principle. The ICJ gives it «great weight» on UNCLOS (§ 338) and cites it by name in the paragraphs of Probe 3 (§§ 138, 276, 281, 294) and in those on cooperation, which are not part of the probe. Whoever sets out the stringent due diligence for the marine environment, or writes «Climate Change, Advisory Opinion, ITLOS Reports 2024», cites ITLOS. (Probe 3)

### 4.2 Inter-American Court, OC-32/25 (29/5/2025), file `oc-32-25.yaml`
Two months earlier, cited by the ICJ only at § 385, in the list. Autonomous right to a healthy climate, «debida diligencia reforzada», future generations, environmental defenders. In the corpus it travels alongside the ICJ in almost every footnote. If the passage bases the right to a healthy climate on the American Convention or uses «OC-32/25», it is ANOTHER_SOURCE. (no probes: human rights and the environment are not probes)

### 4.3 Inter-American Court, OC-23/17 (15/11/2017)
Earlier. Healthy environment as an autonomous right, jurisdiction for transboundary harm. Cited in the corpus alongside the ICJ on the right to the environment; the ICJ does not name it [verified: OC-23 does not appear in the text of the opinion]. (no probe)

### 4.4 ECtHR, KlimaSeniorinnen (9/4/2024)
Earlier, cited by the ICJ at §§ 144 and 385. Art. 8 ECHR, reduced margin on the aims, wide margin on the means, residual budget. Whoever writes «do its part» or cites the margin of appreciation of Strasbourg cites the ECtHR. (no probe)

### 4.5 Human Rights Committee: Teitiota (2019), Billy v. Australia (2022), General Comment No. 36
Earlier, cited in §§ 144, 377, 378, 381. Climate non-refoulement and late adaptation as a violation of Art. 17 come from there. (no probe)

### 4.6 General Assembly, Resolution 76/300 (28 July 2022)
It recognises the right to a clean, healthy and sustainable environment. The ICJ treats it as evidence of the acceptance of the right (§ 392). Those who cite it alone do not take up the advisory opinion. (no probe)

### 4.7 Pulp Mills (2010), Nuclear Weapons (1996), Gabcikovo (1997), Corfu Channel (1949), Trail Smelter (1941), Costa Rica v. Nicaragua (2015, 2018)
Case law on which the Court builds prevention, due diligence, EIA, and compensation for environmental damage. Those who cite them for classic transboundary prevention cite those sources. (probes 3, 5)

### 4.8 ILC Articles on State Responsibility (2001); Chorzow (1928); Armed Activities, Reparations (2022)
The general rules that the Court applies. Anyone who asserts full reparation or a plurality of responsible States without climate cites these sources. (probes 5, 6)

### 4.9 Paris Agreement, decisions 1/CMA.3 (Glasgow) and 1/CMA.5 (first global stocktake), 4/CMA.1
The text and decisions that the Court interprets. Those who cite «highest possible ambition» or the 43% by 2030 from decision 1/CMA.5 cite the treaty or the COP, not the opinion. (probes 1, 2)

### 4.10 IPCC
Source of the science (§§ 72-87, 278, 284, 437). Those who report IPCC data cite the IPCC; those who say that the IPCC is the best available science by naming the ICJ (§ 74) cite the opinion, but outside the probes.

### 4.10-bis Sea-level rise: Pacific Islands Forum (2021) and ILC study group (final report 2025)
The Pacific Islands Forum Declaration on the preservation of maritime zones and the ILC report are invoked by the Court as State practice and convergence on baselines (§§ 360-361). Those who base the stability of maritime zones on those sources cite those sources; the sentence on statehood (§ 363), by contrast, is the Court's own. (probe 7)

### 4.11 Advisory proceedings before the African Court on Human and Peoples' Rights (PALU request, 2025)
Pending. In the corpus, it is the case file that cites the ICJ opinion the most (section 6). The submissions present it as «primary point of reference» for climate obligations in Africa. They are acts of the parties and of friends of the court: Rule 5.6 counts them, «mossa» says so.

## 5. How to recognise a resumption

### 5.1 Strong indications
- A form of `cig-parere-clima.yaml` (title, «ICJ AO», «ICJ Advisory Opinion», «General List No. 187», «Advisory Opinion of 23 July 2025», the French, Spanish, Portuguese, Czech forms) in an act subsequent to 23/7/2025.
- A number from 1 to 457 next to those forms: decipher it with the map.
- The sentences from section 3 or their faithful translations: «agreed primary temperature goal», «highest possible ambition» with «the discretion of parties in the preparation of their NDCs is limited», «stringent» referring to the climate system and not to the marine environment, «quintessentially universal risk», «does not lead to a general exclusion», «substantially complies with the general customary duties», «fossil fuel exploration licences» and «fossil fuel subsidies» as a tort, «not the emission of GHGs per se», «erga omnes» referring to the climate system, «loss of its statehood» with sea-level rise.
- The structure of the opinion: treaties, then custom, then human rights, then responsibility.

### 5.2 False friends
ITLOS (UNCLOS, marine environment, «stringent» for marine pollution); OC-32/25 (autonomous right to a healthy climate, reinforced due diligence); OC-23/17; KlimaSeniorinnen (Art. 8, «do its part»); Urgenda (Arts. 2 and 8 ECHR, its own share); Neubauer (intertemporal freedom); Pulp Mills and Nuclear Weapons (classic prevention); ILC Articles (responsibility in general); Paris and COP decisions (letter of the treaty); IPCC (data); Resolution 76/300 (right to the environment). Polarity: those who argue that the climate treaties are lex specialis, that NDCs are entirely discretionary, that prevention does not apply to climate because the harm is cumulative, that causation is impossible or must be presumed, take up the arguments that the Court rejects (§§ 163, 237, 133, 434), not the advisory opinion; those who reject them with the words of the opinion take it up.

### 5.3 Uncertain cases
- «Advisory Opinions» in the plural, or «the three climate advisory opinions»: this is not a quotation of the ICJ advisory opinion alone; if the proposition is common to all three, flag it as a chain.
- Right to a healthy environment: this is not a probe for this case (a statement shared with OC-32/25, Resolution 76/300, and the UN committees). If an instrument names the ICJ on this point, the citation is recorded by name, without a probe.
- Due diligence «stringent»: if it concerns the marine environment or UNCLOS, it is ITLOS; if it concerns the climate system, NDCs, or domestic measures, it is the advisory opinion.
- Fossil fuels: the ICJ's statement concerns State responsibility (§ 427) and scope (§ 94). An instrument stating that companies must cease extraction without naming the State may only rely on it under rule 5.6.1.
- Before 23/7/2025 no one may rely on the advisory opinion: it is possible to rely on the States' written statements or Resolution 77/276, which are the request and party documents.

### 5.4 Mediated reuse
The sentences that the Court takes from ITLOS («put in place a national system, including legislation, administrative procedures and an enforcement mechanism necessary to regulate the activities in question» at § 281, the «obligation of due diligence is particularly relevant» at § 252, the cumulative effects at § 276, the precaution «integral part» at § 294 from the 2011 Chamber opinion) pass through ITLOS: it cites ITLOS in «fondamento» and signals the reuse as nested. The same applies to Pulp Mills («use all the means at its disposal», §§ 132, 272, 281, 290), to the Human Rights Committee (§§ 377-381) and to the ILC Articles. In the opposite direction, the submissions to the African Court reuse the opinion and put it forward as a source: whoever then reuses those submissions reuses the opinion in nested form.

### 5.5 Who speaks
The name may appear in a brief by a party or an amicus curiae (almost the entire African file, Juliana before the Inter-American Commission, Poelina, Dykstra, Observatorio do Clima), in the descriptive part of a judgment (Greenpeace Nordic § 136), among the arguments of a party as reported by the court (Greenpeace Nordic § 212), in the reasoning adopting it (Bonaire, Notre Affaire a Tous, Belgian Constitutional Court, Lhoimggin), or in a table of sources. State this in the «mossa».

### 5.6 The group's rules on what counts as a holding (decided by the PI on 23/9/2026, applicable to all cases)
1. The same reasoning applied to a private company also counts.
2. It also counts if it is stated by a party, an intervener, or an amicus, not only by the court.
3. It also counts if the court summarises it as a party's argument: it must be flagged as such.
