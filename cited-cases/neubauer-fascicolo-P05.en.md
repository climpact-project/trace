## 0. What it is for and how to read it

You are given a passage from ANOTHER case. You must state whether it takes up a proposition from Neubauer (RIPRESA), whether it asserts something similar based on another source (ALTRA_FONTE), or whether it does not assert it (NON_AFFERMA). Links in the chain of argument: (a) warming is largely irreversible, CO2 accumulates and every tonne emitted consumes a finite remaining budget; (b) the State is required to have a duty of care (Schutzpflicht) for life and health, directed also to the future, towards the living and, only in objective terms, towards future generations, fulfilled through internationally embedded mitigation plus adaptation, and reviewable only at the minimum threshold: here NOT violated; (c) the constitutional climate objective (Art. 20a GG) is a justiciable norm with an international dimension (one cannot evade by pointing to others' emissions), which the legislature has concretised in the Paris target and to which it remains bound until it redefines it in a transparent manner; (d) the temperature target translates into a remaining budget, global then national, which the court does not quantify in figures but of which the legislature must take account with special diligence under uncertainty; (e) the quantities of emissions permitted today have an anticipatory effect similar to an interference (eingriffsähnliche Vorwirkung) on the future freedom of the SAME applicants: this makes the application admissible and opens the merits; (f) justification requires proportionality between generations: no generation may consume a large part of the budget leaving subsequent generations a radical reduction; Art. 20a confirms this; (g) remedy: precautions for a transition that respects freedom, timely orientation and planning certainty, parliamentary legislative reservation, order to the legislature to legislate by 31/12/2022 with the provisions remaining in force; (h) side branch: towards those living abroad the Schutzpflicht is conceivable in principle but of different content and the question is left open.

Five things to always keep in mind.

1. The judgment is a single one, in two languages. The canonical versions are the German ones; foreign courts read the official English, which carries the Randnummern in the margin but omits many Rn. with «[…]» (see 2.1). A reference to «para.», «§», «Rn.», «recital» or «Ziffer» with a number from 1 to 270 is always the same number; «paragraph 2(c) of the operative part» or «headnote 2c» is the Leitsatz, not the operative part.
2. The two halves of the case have opposite signs. The duty of care on climate exists but is NOT violated (Rn. 143-172: review limited to missing measures, manifestly unsuitable, wholly insufficient or seriously deficient ones). The violation lies in future freedom: the emissions permitted until 2030 offload onto the period after a reduction that will have to be drastic (Rn. 182-195, 243-258). Anyone citing Neubauer to say that the protection was insufficient gets the case wrong; anyone citing it for intertemporal freedom cites it correctly.
3. The court fixes no figure. It is not for the courts to translate Art. 20a into degrees or tonnes (Rn. 207); the budget of the Sachverständigenrat (6.7 Gt from 2020 for 1.75 degrees) is a parameter «albeit not precisely quantifiable» and the uncertainties prevent using it as an exact measure (Rn. 229, 236). This is the clear difference from Urgenda, where the 25% minimum is fixed by the court.
4. The duty towards future generations is ONLY objective: those not yet born are not holders of rights (Rn. 109, 146). The Vorwirkung protects the living applicants in their own future, not posterity. It is the reverse of Colombia (STC4360-2018), where future generations are rights-holders.
5. Leitsatz 2c (the State cannot evade by pointing to others' emissions) is `propria_quota` of Urgenda taken up with declared attribution: Rn. 203 cites VG Berlin, BVerwG, Thomson (New Zealand), Gerechtshof Den Haag no. 64, Hoge Raad no. 5.7.7 and the Ninth Circuit. Anyone taking up that point through Neubauer takes up Urgenda in nested form (see 5.4). It is not one of the six probes in this case.

## 1. The ruling

### 1.1 Identity, structure, numbering

Federal Constitutional Court, First Senate, Order of 24 March 2021 (published on 29 April 2021: some texts date it thus, e.g. Sacchi v. Germany, note 17), joined cases 1 BvR 2656/18, 1 BvR 78/20, 1 BvR 96/20, 1 BvR 288/20; ECLI:DE:BVerfG:2021:rs20210324.1bvr265618 (34 documents in the corpus use it); official citation BVerfGE 157, 30 (in 11 documents in the corpus, including KlimaSeniorinnen and the subsequent German appeals). Unanimous (para. 270). Applicants: young Germans (2656/18, 96/20, 288/20), two environmental associations (2656/18, appeal inadmissible), fifteen persons from Bangladesh and Nepal (78/20). Challenged act: the Federal Climate Change Act (KSG) of 12/12/2019, §§ 3(1) and 4(1) with Annex 2 (55% reduction by 2030 compared to 1990 and annual sectoral quantities) and § 4(6) (post-2030 update by regulation).

Structure: Headnotes 1-5 (DE pp. 1-3, EN pp. 1-3); heading and operative part in five points (DE p. 8, EN p. 6); table of contents (DE pp. 8-12, EN pp. 6-11); grounds A-E, paras. 1-270: A facts (paras. 1-89: statute, Paris, EU law, climate science, budget, appeals and observations), B admissibility (paras. 90-142), C merits (paras. 143-265: I duty of protection towards residents, II towards applicants abroad, III intertemporal guarantee of freedom), D consequences (paras. 266-269), E vote (para. 270).

Operative part (EN p. 6): «§ 3(1) second sentence and § 4(1) third sentence of the Federal Climate Change Act of 12 December 2019 (Federal Law Gazette I, p. 2513) in conjunction with Annex 2 are incompatible with fundamental rights insofar as they lack provisions on the updating of reduction targets for periods from 2031 that satisfy the constitutional requirements as set forth in the reasons»; «In all other respects, the constitutional complaints are rejected»; «The legislator must enact provisions by no later than 31 December 2022 on the updating of reduction targets for periods from 2031 as set forth in the reasons». German (DE p. 8): «Der Gesetzgeber ist verpflichtet, spätestens bis zum 31. Dezember 2022 die Fortschreibung der Minderungsziele für Zeiträume ab dem Jahr 2031 nach Maßgabe der Gründe zu regeln». The provisions remain applicable (order for continued validity, paras. 267-268).

### 1.2 Procedural History

Facts (A). The IPCC as a reliable basis (paras. 16-17); quasi-linear relationship between cumulative CO2 and temperature, irreversibility, tipping points (paras. 18-21, 32); effects in Germany and migration (paras. 22-28); Germany at 2% of annual emissions and 1.1% of the population (para. 30); mitigation, negative emissions, adaptation (paras. 33-34); the budget: «this amount is referred to as the “carbon budget” or “CO2 budget”», with the Sachverständigenrat (SRU) calculation for 1.75 degrees at 67% (para. 36); almost every conduct emits CO2, therefore climate neutrality requires «fundamental changes and restrictions» in ways of living (para. 37). This is followed by a summary of the four complaints and the observations of the Bundestag and the Government (paras. 38-89), largely omitted in the English version.

Admissibility (B). Only the provisions of the KSG are admissible as the subject matter (paras. 90-95). Standing for the duty of protection (Schutzpflicht) under Art. 2(2) and 14(1) GG: possible violation because the climate is global but «this does not render it impossible or superfluous for Germany to make its own contribution» (para. 99); also the applicants from Bangladesh and Nepal, because the effectiveness of fundamental rights towards them is not excluded a priori (para. 101); the imminence of the injury even if future, when the course of events is no longer correctable (para. 108); no rights of the unborn: «The complainants are not asserting the rights of unborn persons or even of entire future generations, neither of whom enjoy subjective fundamental rights» (para. 109; DE p. 53: «Diesen kommt keine subjektive Grundrechtsberechtigung zu»); it is not an actio popularis (para. 110). Art. 20a does not confer standing; it is a Staatszielbestimmung without subjective rights, albeit justiciable (para. 112). The right to an ecological minimum subsistence level («ecological minimum standard of living») and the «right to a future consistent with human dignity» remain undecided because they are not violated (paras. 113-115). Then the central construction (paras. 116-134): the quantities up to 2030 already determine future restrictions, «not just in factual terms, but with advance legal effects» (para. 118); every emitted CO2 is irreversible and leaves a remaining budget (para. 119); the Paris target, as a concretization of Art. 20a, corresponds to a national budget «albeit not precisely quantifiable», and «The restrictions on freedom that will be necessary in the future are thus already built into the generosity of the current climate change legislation» (para. 120); the time remaining determines the severity of restrictions, with reference to Hoge Raad 7.4.3 (para. 121); fundamental rights can apply «as intertemporal guarantees of freedom» (para. 122, canonical VW); the applicants are currently, individually and directly affected (paras. 129-133), but not those abroad, who are not subject to German measures (para. 132). Associations have no standing as «advocates of nature» (paras. 136-137). EU law does not preclude (para. 141).

Merits I: Duty of protection towards residents (paras. 143-172). Art. 2(2) GG obliges protection from climate change with measures that «with the help of international involvement» contribute to stopping warming, complemented by adaptation (para. 144). The duty is future-oriented and can establish protection for future generations «solely objective» (para. 146, canonical SP). The ECHR (Öneryıldız, Budayeva, Cordella) does not provide broader protection (para. 147). The duty covers climate risks, towards the living and objectively towards posterity (para. 148); the impossibility of stopping climate change alone «does not, in principle, rule out the possibility of a duty of protection arising from fundamental rights», but requires internationally oriented action (para. 149); adaptation is the other pillar (para. 150). Limited review (Untermaßverbot): violation only «if no precautionary measures whatsoever have been taken, or if the adopted provisions and measures prove to be manifestly unsuitable or completely inadequate for achieving the required protection goal, or if the provisions and measures fall significantly short of the protection goal» (para. 152). This is not the case: the law aims at climate neutrality and reduces continuously (paras. 154-156); entirely insufficient would be only letting climate change run its course «using nothing but adaptation measures», with reference to Rechtbank 4.75 and Hoge Raad 7.5.2 (para. 157); the Paris target is not outside the margin of appreciation, 1.5 is preferable due to tipping points (Hoge Raad 4.2 and 4.4, Irish Supreme Court 3.7) but uncertainty leaves a margin (paras. 159-163); adaptation complements the protection of health (paras. 164-165); the path to 2030, perhaps still calibrated to 2 degrees, does not violate the duty of protection but raises the question of Art. 20a and post-2030 reductions (paras. 166-168); the implementation deficit is recoverable (paras. 169-170); same outcome for property under Art. 14(1) (paras. 171-172).

Merits II: the applicants from Bangladesh and Nepal (paras. 173-181). «Although it does appear conceivable in principle, there is no need to decide at this point» whether the duty of protection applies to them; German emissions are 2% and limiting them «is for the German legislator» (para. 174, canonical PE); the binding effect of fundamental rights is not confined to the territory (BND, para. 175), and a possible link is the contribution, albeit small, of German emissions (end of para. 175); but such a duty «would not in any case have the same content as that vis-à-vis people in Germany» (para. 176), because abroad «it is practically impossible for the German state to afford protection to people living abroad by implementing adaptation measures there» (para. 178); political and international responsibility remains, Art. 9(1) and 2(2) Paris (para. 179); in any case not violated, and the criterion «significantly short» is not applicable by isolating mitigation from adaptation (paras. 180-181).

Merits III: the intertemporal guarantee of freedom (paras. 182-265). Violation: the precautions to manage post-2030 reductions in a rights-respecting manner are lacking (para. 182). The permitted quantities have «an advance interference-like effect (eingriffsähnliche Vorwirkung)» and call for constitutional justification (para. 183, canonical bis VW); all freedom is protected, including the freedom that emits (para. 184); but it encounters the limit of Article 20a, concretized in the Paris target (para. 185); «every amount of CO2 that is allowed today irreversibly depletes the remaining budget that was predetermined in accordance with constitutional law» (para. 186); the effect is produced precisely by the challenged provisions and «operates not only de facto but also de jure» (para. 187). Justification in two steps: compatibility with Article 20a (paras. 189-190; the objective Schutzpflicht towards future generations adds nothing, para. 191, German only) and proportionality (paras. 192-194): «It follows from the principle of proportionality that one generation must not be allowed to consume large portions of the CO2 budget while bearing a relatively minor share of the reduction effort, if this would involve leaving subsequent generations with a drastic reduction burden and expose their lives to serious losses of freedom» (para. 192, canonical PI); Article 20a confirms this: posterity must not be forced to «radical abstinence» (para. 193); «It is thus imperative to prevent an overly short-sighted and thus one-sided distribution of freedom and reduction burdens to the detriment of the future» and the risk must be contained already today (para. 194). Article 20a: obliges climate protection up to climate neutrality, without absolute precedence but with increasing weight (para. 198); international dimension: «No state can stop global warming on its own. Furthermore, emissions from every state contribute to climate change in the same way», with Rechtbank 4.90 (para. 200); duty to seek and implement international solutions (para. 201); the causation objection fails: «the obligation to take national climate action cannot be invalidated by arguing that such action would be incapable of stopping climate change» (para. 202); «The state may not evade its responsibility here by pointing to greenhouse gas emissions in other states», and it must not create incentives to undermine cooperation (para. 203); Paris «very much relies on mutual trust» (para. 204). Justiciability: «Art. 20a GG is a justiciable legal provision», not «a non-binding proclamation» (para. 205, canonical AG); the constraint of the political process exists because democracy is organized «along more short-term lines based on election cycles» and posterity «naturally have no voice of their own in shaping the current political agenda» (para. 206); however, «It is not, in principle, for the courts to translate the open wording of Art. 20a GG into quantifiable global warming limits and corresponding emission amounts or reduction targets», without Article 20a being beyond review (para. 207). Concretization: the target of § 1 sentence 3 KSG (well below 2, possibly 1.5) is the constitutional measure (paras. 208-210), within the margin but revisable in light of new knowledge, and a regression would have to be justified (paras. 211-212); «Unless the legislator redefines the fundamental climate goal in a recognisable way and in a transparent process, it must be held to its own specification of the goal arising from constitutional law» (para. 213, canonical bis AG). Budget as a parameter: temperature must be translated into emissions using the IPCC budget approach (para. 215); two steps, global budget then «the specific remaining national CO2 budget» (para. 216); the Government does not deny the validity of the approach, and without an idea of the total quantity one would engage in «Klimaschutz ins Blaue hinein» (paras. 217-218, German only, with Hoge Raad 2.1, 4.6, 7.4.3 and Irish Court 4.3); IPCC and SRU figures (para. 219); uncertainties in both directions (paras. 220-228); the national share cannot be determined with exactitude, but this does not make it arbitrary: «Nor can a specific constitutional obligation to reduce CO2 emissions be invalidated by simply arguing that Germany’s share of the reduction burden and of the global CO2 budget are impossible to determine» (para. 225, with CBDR); under uncertainty Article 20a «imposes a special duty of care» and requires taking into account reliable indications of serious or irreversible damage, also under Article 3(3) UNFCCC (para. 229). Application: the budget of 6.7 Gt would be almost exhausted by 2030 (paras. 231-235), but the uncertainties are «currently too great to allow the budget size calculated by the Advisory Council to serve as an exact numerical benchmark for review by the Federal Constitutional Court» (paras. 236-237); no autonomous duty of investigation or reasoning (paras. 239-241); the commitment to 1.5 remains (para. 242). Proportionality applied: the duty to distribute reductions over time is violated «in a forward-looking manner that respects fundamental rights» (para. 243); after 2030 the SRU budget would barely suffice for another year (para. 246), the exact measure is uncertain but the risk requires precautions (para. 247); «respecting future freedom requires that the transition to climate neutrality be initiated in good time», with orientation, «developmental urgency and planning certainty» (paras. 248-250); § 4(6) KSG defers everything to a 2025 regulation: «it is imperative under constitutional law that further reduction targets beyond 2030 are specified in good time, extending sufficiently far into the future», with the Irish Court 6.45 et seq. (paras. 251-255); § 4(6) is insufficient and violates the reservation of law under Article 80(1) GG: the quantities are set by parliament, a mere approval by the Bundestag is not enough (paras. 256-265; Headnote 5). Consequences: incompatibility with provisional effect and a deadline for the legislature (paras. 266-268).

### 1.3 Legal Basis

Only the Grundgesetz: Art. 2(2) S. 1 and 14(1) (protective duties), Art. 2(1) and special freedoms (Vorwirkung), Art. 20a (climate, future generations), Art. 80(1) and the rule of law reservation, Art. 1(3) (extraterritorial binding). The ECHR appears only as confirmation that it does not go further (Rn. 99, 147). Paris enters through § 1 S. 3 KSG as concretization of the target (Rn. 208-210) and as a network of mutual trust (Rn. 203-204, 225); UNFCCC Art. 3 for CBDR and precaution (Rn. 225, 229). IPCC and SRU as the factual basis of the budget (Rn. 16-17, 36, 216-229). Its own case law: Kalkar (BVerfGE 49, 89) for the duty directed at the future and the risks of implementation (Rn. 108, 146, 194), BND (BVerfGE 154, 152) for the binding abroad (Rn. 175, 176), Emissionshandel (BVerfGE 118, 79) for Art. 20a (Rn. 198, 205). Foreign case law only as reinforcement: Urgenda (Rn. 121, 157, 161, 200, 203, 218), Irish Supreme Court 31/7/2020 (Rn. 161, 218, 253, 262), VG Berlin 31/10/2019 (Rn. 110, 148, 203), Thomson (Rn. 203), Ninth Circuit Juliana (Rn. 203), Carvalho of the EU Tribunal (Rn. 110).

### 1.5 Boundary points

- Future generations: duty ONLY objective (Rn. 109, 146, 148; Leitsatz 1 «objective duty»). Those who attribute rights to the unborn do not take up Neubauer on that very point; they may take up Colombia.
- Vorwirkung: on the same applicants in the future (Rn. 122, 131: «They are themselves capable of experiencing the measures necessary to reduce CO2 emissions after 2030»). It is a defensive position against future restrictions by the State, not a Schutzpflicht (the BVerfG itself will say so on 18/1/2022, see 4.23).
- No judicial figure (Rn. 207, 229, 236). The budget is a parameter, not an order. Those who write that Neubauer imposed a percentage or a budget are wrong; those who write that the 2021 law raised the target to 65% describe the legislator's response (thus Engels v. Germany, § 3).
- Schutzpflicht not violated (Tenor 3; Rn. 151, 172, 180). A text that uses Neubauer to establish the inadequacy of State protection takes up at most the standard of review (Rn. 152) or the standard of admissibility (Rn. 99).
- Leitsatz 2c and Rn. 203: Urgenda's own_quota with declared authorship. Section 5.4 applies.
- Abroad: open (Rn. 174), different (Rn. 176-178), not violated (Rn. 180-181). Duarte Agostinho describes it as the only national case in which extraterritoriality was raised (§ 64) and then decides in the negative on its own behalf.

### 2.1 How to read a reference

- The Randnummern are the same in both languages. In the German PDF, the marginal number is at the beginning of the paragraph; in the English one, the position is shifted by page in some cases straddling (e.g., Rn. 198, 201, 203) and two German Rn. may be merged into one English paragraph (Rn. 205-206, 206-207): the boundary is determined by the German text.
- The English omits with «[…]»: Rn. 41-46, 48-53, 56-58, 61-66, 72-74, 80-84, 86-88 (party observations), 93-95, 105-107, 124-125, 134, 139-140, 191, 217-218, 240-241, 257-258, 262-264, 267-269, in addition to doctrinal references within many Rn. A reference to one of these Rn. comes from the German (or from a private translation, such as the French one used in Belgium).
- «Leitsatz» / «headnote» / «paragraph 2(c) of the operative part» (as the Prague Municipal Court and the Czech Supreme Court put it) = holdings 1-5, not the Tenor. The Tenor is the list 1-5 after «held on 24 March 2021» (EN p. 6, DE p. 8).
- Three-digit numbers between 1 and 270 = Rn.; «Rn. 1-270» in a table of authorities (La Rose, Canadian Federal Court of Appeal) is the citation of the entire decision. «Rz» is the abbreviation used by German civil courts (LG München, DUH v. BMW). «§ 36 de l'arrêt» in the Belgian judgment is Rn. 36 (the SRU calculation at 67%).
- Dates: 24/3/2021 is the decision, 29/4/2021 the publication. Before 24/3/2021, only the five complaints (2018-2020, party documents of the corpus: `complaint_0dcc`, `_d92e`, `_ba67`, `_027e`, `_ded4`) may be relied upon: whoever does so relies on the applicants, e.g., the «Vollbremsung» (Rn. 126, 192) and the per capita budget of 3.465 Gt (heading, EN p. 5).
- Recurring errors in citing texts: Sacchi dates the decision «29 April 2021» (note 17); Juliana (brief in Spanish) writes «BvR 2656/18/1»; Lliuya v. RWE «24.C)3.2021» (OCR); OC-32/25 refers to «párrs. 246 y 265» for reduction orders and to «párrs. 40 y 110» for diffuse standing. A wrong number in a reference remains a reference to Neubauer.

## 3. The most frequently quoted phrases, verbatim

Official English translation `order_7977` (foreign courts cite this one) and, for the terms that travel in German, the original `order_c68f`.

1. Headnote 1, EN p. 1: «The state’s duty of protection arising from Art. 2(2) first sentence of the Basic Law also encompasses the duty to protect life and health against the risks posed by climate change. It can furthermore give rise to an objective duty to protect future generations.»
2. Headnote 2b, EN p. 1: «a special duty of care imposed upon the legislator by Art. 20a of the Basic Law – also for the benefit of future generations – entails an obligation to take account of sufficiently reliable indications pointing to the possibility of serious or irreversible impairments.»
3. Headnote 2c, EN p. 2: «The fact that no state can resolve the problems of climate change on its own due to the global nature of the climate and global warming does not invalidate the national obligation to take climate action.» and «The state cannot evade its responsibility by pointing to greenhouse gas emissions in other states.» (DE p. 2: «Der Staat kann sich seiner Verantwortung nicht durch den Hinweis auf die Treibhausgasemissionen in anderen Staaten entziehen.»)
4. Headnote 2e, EN p. 2: «Art. 20a of the Basic Law is a justiciable legal provision designed to commit the political process to a favouring of ecological interests, partly with a view to future generations.»
5. Headnote 4, EN p. 2: «In their subjective dimension, fundamental rights – as intertemporal guarantees of freedom – afford protection against the greenhouse gas reduction burdens imposed by Art. 20a of the Basic Law being unilaterally offloaded onto the future.» (DE p. 2: «Subjektivrechtlich schützen die Grundrechte als intertemporale Freiheitssicherung vor einer einseitigen Verlagerung der durch Art. 20a GG aufgegebenen Treibhausgasminderungslast in die Zukunft.»)
6. Headnote 4, EN p. 2: «future generations who wish to carry on preserving these foundations are not forced to engage in radical abstinence.» (DE: «nicht nur um den Preis radikaler eigener Enthaltsamkeit weiter bewahren könnten»)
7. Para. 120, EN p. 37: «The restrictions on freedom that will be necessary in the future are thus already built into the generosity of the current climate change legislation.»
8. Para. 122, EN p. 38: «Every consumed part of the CO2 allowance reduces the remaining budget, narrows the possibilities for any other CO2-relevant exercise of freedom and shortens the time left for initiating and completing a socio-technological transformation.»
9. Para. 146, EN p. 42: «However, this duty to afford intergenerational protection has a solely objective dimension because future generations – either as a whole or as the sum of individuals not yet born – do not yet carry any fundamental rights in the present» (quoted, translated into Spanish, in OC-32/25, p. 112).
10. Para. 149, EN p. 43: «The fact that the German state is incapable of halting climate change on its own and is reliant upon international involvement because of climate change’s global impact and the global nature of its causes does not, in principle, rule out the possibility of a duty of protection arising from fundamental rights»
11. Para. 152, EN p. 44: «manifestly unsuitable or completely inadequate for achieving the required protection goal»
12. Para. 174, EN p. 50: «Although it does appear conceivable in principle, there is no need to decide at this point whether duties of protection arising from fundamental rights also place the German state under an obligation vis-à-vis the complainants living in Bangladesh and in Nepal» (quoted in full in Duarte Agostinho, § 65).
13. Para. 183, EN p. 53: «has an advance interference-like effect (eingriffsähnliche Vorwirkung) on the freedom of the complainants – freedom that is comprehensively protected under the Basic Law.»
14. Para. 186, EN p. 54: «every amount of CO2 that is allowed today irreversibly depletes the remaining budget that was predetermined in accordance with constitutional law»

16. Para. 192, EN p. 56: «one generation must not be allowed to consume large portions of the CO2 budget while bearing a relatively minor share of the reduction effort, if this would involve leaving subsequent generations with a drastic reduction burden and expose their lives to serious losses of freedom» (DE p. 81: «radikale Reduktionslast»; the «Vollbremsung» is the applicants' wording).
17. Para. 194, EN p. 57: «It is thus imperative to prevent an overly short-sighted and thus one-sided distribution of freedom and reduction burdens to the detriment of the future.»

20. Para. 205, EN p. 61: «Art. 20a GG is a justiciable legal provision.»; «this does not mean that Art. 20a GG is a non-binding proclamation».
21. Para. 206, EN p. 61: «because the democratic political process is organised along more short-term lines based on election cycles» and «future generations - those who will be most affected - naturally have no voice of their own in shaping the current political agenda».

## 4. The world around: competing sources

For each one: what it states, on what basis, how it is recognised, whether it cites Neubauer. The probes at issue are in parentheses.

### 4.1 Urgenda (Rechtbank 24/6/2015, Hof 9/10/2018, Hoge Raad 20/12/2019)

`urgenda-foundation-v-state-of-the-netherlands-decision_f1ad`, `..._decision_8a5e`, `..._judgment_ed17`. Via civil law (Art. 6:162 BW) in 2015, Arts. 2 and 8 ECHR from 2018; minimum of 25% by 2020 derived from scientific and international consensus and ORDERED by the court (HR 6.4: «can in any case be regarded as the State’s minimum fair share»; 7.5.1 «an absolute minimum»); order as to result with freedom of means (HR 8.2.6). It differs from Neubauer on the whole method: conventional rather than constitutional law, duty towards living residents rather than intertemporal freedom, judicial quantification rather than budget as parameter, order to reduce rather than order to legislate. It coincides on the point of the share: HR 5.7.7 («a country could easily evade its partial responsibility by pointing out other countries or its own small share») and Hof 62 are cited at Rn. 203; Rechtbank 4.90 at Rn. 200; 4.75 and HR 7.5.2 at Rn. 157; HR 4.2 and 4.4 at Rn. 161; HR 7.4.3 at Rn. 121; HR 2.1, 4.6, 7.4.3 at Rn. 218 (German only). It is recognized by: «its part», «minimum fair share», 25%, «real and immediate risk», Arts. 2 and 8 ECHR, non-legislative order. Earlier: it cannot cite Neubauer. Rule: whoever bases their own share on Urgenda with Neubauer alongside takes up Urgenda; whoever bases intertemporal freedom or the budget on Urgenda mistakes the source, and the take-up is of Neubauer.

### 4.2 Verein KlimaSeniorinnen Schweiz v. Switzerland (ECtHR, GC, 9/4/2024, no. 53600/20)

`verein-klimaseniorinnen-schweiz-and-others-v-switzerland-judgment_f4b1`. Under Art. 8 ECHR: duty of «do its part» (§ 545); reduced margin on objectives, wide margin on means (§ 543); five requirements including «the overall remaining carbon budget for the same time frame, or another equivalent method of quantification of future GHG emissions» (§ 550(a), p. 207); «to avoid a disproportionate burden on future generations, immediate action needs to be taken and adequate intermediate reduction goals must be set for the period leading to net neutrality» (§ 549, p. 207); individual victims only with intense exposure, associations under conditions (§§ 487-502); no order. Cites Neubauer: in the descriptive part §§ 254-257 (pp. 115-117, with the translated headnotes), § 408 (p. 167: the interveners speak of «the anticipatory prevention of future interference, which was a concept developed by the German Constitutional Court in its climate change case-law»), and in the assessment at § 571 (p. 212): «The Court also finds convincing the reasoning of the GFCC, which rejected the argument that it was impossible to determine the national carbon budget» with reference to «paragraphs 215-29». The separate opinion of Eicke cites Rn. 204 for the mutual trust of Paris (p. 238) and Art. 20a as what is missing from the ECHR (p. 249). Effect for the classification: from 9/4/2024 the residual budget as a requirement can be attributed to Strasbourg; if the text cites KlimaSeniorinnen § 571 or § 550 without Neubauer, it is ANOTHER_SOURCE with a nesting note on BC (see 5.4); the «disproportionate burden on future generations» of § 549 is close to PI but founded on Art. 8, without Vorwirkung nor generations as rights-holders. It is recognized from 53600/20, «do its part», «real prospect», «pressing need». (BC, PI)

### 4.3 Duarte Agostinho v. Portugal and 32 States (ECtHR, GC, 9/4/2024, inadmissibility)

`duarte-agostinho-and-others-v-portugal-and-32-other-states-decision_554b`. Jurisdiction only of Portugal, no extraterritorial extension by way of interpretation (§ 213), failure to exhaust domestic remedies. It cites Neubauer only in the review: «The only case in which an issue of extraterritoriality arose was Neubauer and Others v. Federal Republic of Germany» (§ 64, p. 29) and quotes in full paras. 174-178 (§ 65, pp. 30-31). It is the source that DECIDES in negative terms what Neubauer leaves open: whoever denies the protection of those abroad using the language of «jurisdiction» under Art. 1 ECHR follows Duarte (PE polarity); whoever says «conceivable in principle» but «would not in any case have the same content» follows Neubauer. (PE)

### 4.4 Greenpeace Nordic

(a) Norwegian Supreme Court, 22/12/2020, `greenpeace-nordic-assn-v-ministry-of-petroleum-and-energy-people-v-arctic-oil-judgment_2f86`: prior to Neubauer; Art. 112 of the Constitution as «safety valve» (§ 142), territorial responsibility for combustion (§ 159), Urgenda distinguished (§ 173). Norway, 28/10/2025, `greenpeace-nordic-and-others-v-norway-judgment_86b9`: only procedural obligations, impact assessment before authorisation (§ 318). ### 4.5 Leghari v. Pakistan (Lahore High Court, 2015 and 2018)

`leghari-v-federation-of-pakistan-decision_0d83`, `..._judgment_5be2`. Antecedent. Constitutional life and dignity against «the delay and lethargy of the State in implementing the Framework» (0d83, § 8); managerial remedy. Close to AG only for the binding effect on the adopted policy, in terms of implementation and not of justiciability of a constitutional objective (excluded `prov_leghari_inattuazione`). Does not cite Neubauer. (AG)

### 4.6 Juliana v. United States (D. Or. 2016; Ninth Circuit 17/1/2020)

Prior. Right to «a climate system capable of sustaining human life» (Aiken, p. 32) and public trust; Ninth Circuit: remedy beyond judicial power. The Ninth Circuit is cited by Neubauer at Rn. 203 (pp. 19-20 of the opinion) on the point of the share, together with Urgenda. The Staton dissent brings the idea that every reduction counts. The subsequent briefs in the Juliana docket (e.g., the brief in Spanish `juliana-youth-...__0206998a`, pp. 77 and 112) cite Neubauer in a chain with Urgenda and KlimaSeniorinnen. Recognition is given to two proceedings, danger creation, redressability, Article III. (VW by contrast: those who base the later proceedings on public trust are `prov_juliana_public_trust`)

### 4.7 Sacchi v. Argentina and others (UN Committee on the Rights of the Child, 22/9/2021)

`sacchi-et-al-v-argentina-et-al-decision_1009` (Germany). Extraterritorial jurisdiction under the OC-23/17 test (effective control, foreseeability); inadmissible for failure to exhaust domestic remedies. Cites Neubauer three times: the authors (§ 7.3, p. 10, to argue that the constitutional appeal would not provide an effective remedy), the State (§ 7.5, p. 11), the Committee (§ 9.17, p. 15: the German Court «admitted claims against the Federal Climate Protection Act submitted by children who were neither nationals nor residents in the State party» and «specifically emphasized the need for the State party authorities to engage in internationally oriented activities to tackle climate change at the global level», note 32 to Leitsatz 2c). This is the use of Neubauer for the exhaustion of remedies and for the admissibility of foreign claimants: whoever relies on it in this way relies on Rn. 101 and Leitsatz 2c, not on the merits of the PE. (PE)

### 4.8 Milieudefensie v. Royal Dutch Shell (The Hague District Court, 26/5/2021; Court of Appeal, 12/11/2024)

`milieudefensie-et-al-v-royal-dutch-shell-plc-judgment_4679`, `..._7bfb`. Duty of care of a company (Art. 6:162 BW, UNGP), -45% by 2030 in 2021, overturned in 2024. Neither judgment cites Neubauer (the first is from two months later). False friend for PI and BC: the budget and reduction pathways are used for a company and with its own sources (IPCC, IEA). Under the group rule (5.6), a reason from Neubauer applied to a company counts as a reuse, but here the reason is absent. In the corpus, Neubauer enters litigation against companies with the citations against ING and with the German cases (4.23). (PI, BC)

### 4.9 Commune de Grande-Synthe v. France (Conseil d'Etat 19/11/2020 and 1/7/2021)

`municipality-of-grande-synthe-v-france-decision_7eca`, `..._cb9c`, `..._opinion_fd32`. Review of the governmental refusal regarding the trajectory set by law (-40% by 2030), injunction to «toutes mesures utiles». Does not cite Neubauer. Close to AG in the second move (binding of the legislature to its own concretization) but without constitutional justiciability nor electoral cycles; close to Leitsatz 4 on time, without intertemporal freedom. (AG)

### 4.10 Inter-American Court, OC-23/17 (15/11/2017)

Prior. Jurisdiction of the State of origin due to the causal link with damage outside the territory (§ 101). This is the positive route to extraterritoriality, which Sacchi applies: whoever grounds a duty towards persons abroad on effective control and foreseeability draws on OC-23/17 or Sacchi, not Neubauer (excluded `prov_sacchi_giurisdizione`). (PE)

### 4.11 ITLOS, Advisory Opinion 21/5/2024 (COSIS)

`...-international-law-opinion_4f32`. UNCLOS Part XII, due diligence «stringent» (para. 243), obligation of conduct (para. 233). It does not cite Neubauer (three written statements in the file do). Far from the probes. (none)

### 4.12 ICJ, Obligations of States in respect of Climate Change (23/7/2025)

`...-climate-change-decision_af16`. Treaties and custom, due diligence «stringent» (§ 138), 1.5 as «primary temperature goal» (§ 224), NDCs with «highest possible ambition», plural responsibility (§§ 429-431). It does not cite Neubauer (nine written pleadings from the folder in favor). Close to BC for the translation of the target into a duty of conduct, but interstate, without a national budget as a constitutional parameter. (BC)

### 4.13 Inter-American Court, OC-32/25 (29/5/2025)

`..._decision_28df` (Spanish), `..._9329` (English). «Debida diligencia reforzada» (§ 233), «clima sano» (§ 300), «meta de mitigación» (§ 324) with budget as «punto de partida mínimo» (§ 326), future generations. It cites Neubauer only in footnotes: note 27 (p. 14, «párrs. 16 y 17», the IPCC as a reliable basis), note 385 (p. 71, European list), note 394 (p. 72, «párrs. 246 y 265», with the Urgenda District Court, for the mitigation targets), the note on p. 112 translating para. 146 on the duty «exclusivamente objetiva» towards posterity, note 570 (p. 114, «párrs. 40 y 110», standing of many). It is the source that, after 2025, can reattribute to itself the protection of future generations (SP in its own point: OC-32/25 uses Neubauer for the objective duty, but constructs an autonomous right on its own account). (SP, BC)

### 4.14 Massachusetts v. EPA (U.S. Supreme Court, 2007)

Prior. «A reduction in domestic emissions would slow the pace of global emissions increases, no matter what happens elsewhere» (p. 29). American source of the point on the share; unrelated to the Neubauer probes. (none)

### 4.15 Future Generations v. Ministry of the Environment (Supreme Court of Colombia, STC4360-2018, 5/4/2018)

`future-generations-v-ministry-of-the-environment-and-others-decision_7519`. Prior. Environmental rights of future generations as a binding legal relationship, «prestación de “no-hacer”» which limits the freedom of present generations (p. 21); heritage to be handed down (T-411/1992, p. 28); Amazon subject of rights. It is the polarity of the very point of SP (holders of the obligation) and the other way to PI (duty of care, non-proportionality). It is recognized by «generaciones futuras», «sujeto de derechos», «solidaridad». (SP, PI, VW)

### 4.16 The case law of the Bundesverfassungsgericht cited

Kalkar, BVerfGE 49, 89 (1978): duty of protection directed at the future and norms that become harmful in their implementation (Rn. 108, 146, 194); the «Gedanke dynamischen Grundrechtsschutzes» (Leitsatz 5, Rn. 262). BND, BVerfGE 154, 152 (2020): binding of rights outside the territory with differentiated content (Rn. 175-176). Emissionshandel, BVerfGE 118, 79 (2007): Art. 20a binds the legislature (Rn. 198, 205). Rule: whoever sets out the duty directed at the future citing Kalkar, or the binding abroad citing BND, takes up those (excluding `prov_kalkar_futuro`, `prov_bnd_vincolo_estero`); whoever applies them to climate with the words of Neubauer takes up Neubauer. Texts of the three decisions [not verified in the corpus].

### 4.17 ECtHR environmental case law: Öneryıldız, Budayeva, Cordella

Cited at paras. 99 and 147 to argue that the ECHR does not protect beyond Art. 2(2) GG. They are the matrix of positive obligations (excluding `prov_cedu_obblighi_positivi`), the same as in Urgenda and KlimaSeniorinnen. Those who ground the climate duty on Arts. 2 and 8 ECHR do not take up SP; those who ground it on a constitutional provision on life and health «regardless of who or what circumstances are the cause», with the objective duty towards future generations and limited review, take up Neubauer. Distinctive lexicon: «duty of protection» (Schutzpflicht) versus «positive obligation» (ECHR) and «duty of care» (Urgenda, Leghari). Texts of the three judgments [not verified in the corpus]. (SP)

### 4.18 IPCC SR1.5 and the German Advisory Council on the Environment (Sachverständigenrat für Umweltfragen)

The budget as a concept and in its figures (420 and 1170 Gt globally from 2018; 6.7 Gt for Germany from 2020, for 1.75 degrees at 67%) comes from the IPCC and the SRU (paras. 36, 219). Those who report the budget in gigatonnes draw on the IPCC (excluding `prov_ipcc_budget`); those who turn it into a parameter of constitutional review with the double clause (not precisely quantifiable, but requiring special diligence regarding reliable indicators) draw on Neubauer. KlimaSeniorinnen § 550(a) and the Municipal Court of Prague have made the budget a legal requirement through other means. IPCC and SRU texts [not verified in the corpus]. (BC)

### 4.19 Paris Agreement, UNFCCC, CBDR

Paris enters as the concretisation of the target chosen by the legislature (§ 1 S. 3 KSG, paras. 208-210), as a network of trust (paras. 203-204) and as a source of apportionment criteria with the CBDR (para. 225, with art. 3 UNFCCC; excluded `prov_cbdr_ripartizione`); art. 3(3) UNFCCC for precaution (para. 229). Rule: the 2/1.5-degree threshold stated by itself is Paris; the CBDR used to DIFFERENTIATE is UNFCCC; the target binding the legislature «to its own specification» is Neubauer (AG); the conversion of the target into a national budget with a non-arbitrary margin is Neubauer (BC). Neubauer does not use «highest possible ambition» nor the NDCs as a parameter. (AG, BC)

### 4.20 The foreign decisions cited by the Beschluss

VG Berlin 31/10/2019, 10 K 412.18 (Rn. 110, 148, 203): present in the corpus only through citations. BVerwG 30/6/2005, 7 C 26/04 (Rn. 203): idem. Thomson v. Minister for Climate Change Issues (High Court NZ, 2/11/2017, `thomson-v-minister-for-climate-change-issues-decision_32f9`), [133]: the national courts do not treat the issue as «no go» «because the problem is a global one and one country’s efforts alone cannot prevent harm to that country’s people and their environment». Friends of the Irish Environment v. Government of Ireland (Supreme Court, 31/7/2020, folder `friends-of-the-irish-environment-v-the-government-of-ireland-et-al_c212`), cited at Rn. 161, 218, 253, 262 for tipping points, budget and timely orientation of the pathway: it is earlier and concurs with Neubauer on planning certainty (Leitsatz 4, Rn. 248-255) on the basis of ordinary legislation. Ninth Circuit Juliana (Rn. 203). Whoever takes up the point of the quota from VG Berlin, Thomson or the Ninth Circuit takes up those; with Urgenda alongside, Urgenda.

### 4.21 Klimatická žaloba ČR v. Czech Republic (Prague Municipal Court, 15 June 2022; Supreme Administrative Court, 20 February 2023)

`klimaticka-zaloba-cr-v-czech-republic-judgment_f1a4` (English), `..._decision_3404` (Czech), `..._decision_b042` (NSS, 9 As 116/2022, Czech), `..._appeal_e825` (appeal). The Municipal Court orders measures for a -55% target by 2030 and derives from foreign case law that the State does not discharge its obligation through a lesser contribution; Neubauer appears in the applicants' arguments (§§ 81-83, pp. 21-22: «paragraph 2(c) of the operative part», «paragraph 4 of the operative part», «para. 194»). The Supreme Administrative Court sets aside the judgment; the phrase «Koncept uhlíkového rozpočtu jako vyjádření povinností státu plynoucích z Pařížské dohody přijal také první senát německého Spolkového ústavního soudu» (decision_b042, [73], p. 22) appears in Part III.i, which summarises the applicants' appeal on points of law, not in the Court's assessment (Part IV, from p. 25). Who is speaking: the parties. (BC, Leitsatz 2c)

### 4.22 VZW Klimaatzaak v. Belgium (Brussels Court of Appeal, 30/11/2023)

`vzw-klimaatzaak-v-kingdom-of-belgium-others-judgment_e65a` (English), `..._ccce` (French), `..._330b` (Dutch). Articles 2 and 8 ECHR and civil liability; orders a -55% by 2030. It is the foreign court that most uses Neubauer as its own reasoning, from a partial French translation «uncontested»: § 155 (p. 92) on the insufficient ambition today that restricts rights «far more radically in the future»; § 160 (p. 95): the German Court «rightly» stated that the State cannot evade by pointing to others' emissions, and that it follows «a constitutional imperative to actually take own, and if possible internationally agreed, measures to protect the climate» («§203»), immediately next to the Hoge Raad; § 195 (p. 112): «In its Neubauer judgment, the German Court did not comment specifically on the choice of the 67% scenario» (distinguishes); § 201 (p. 115): Neubauer held 55% compatible with Article 2(2) GG (§§ 144-170); § 234 (p. 134): the «price of procrastination» with Rn. 248 et seq.; § 248 (p. 144): the global character does not remove the individual obligation, «As emphasized in the Neubauer judgment»; p. 146 summarizes the appellants' arguments on future freedom. Declared reliance on Leitsatz 2c and Rn. 248 et seq.; distinction on the budget. (PI, BC, Leitsatz 2c)

### 4.23 The German aftermath: Länder, companies, Strasbourg

BVerfG, 1st Chamber, 18/1/2022, 1 BvR 1565/21 and others (`marlene-lemme-et-al-v-state-of-bayern-constitutional-claim-order`, German): applies Neubauer to the appeals against the Länder and rejects them: «Eine eingriffsähnliche Vorwirkung setzt danach aber voraus, dass der jeweilige Gesetzgeber selbst einem grob erkennbaren Budget insgesamt noch zulassungsfähiger CO2-Emissionen unterliegt» (p. 9); for the Länder «ist keine wenigstens grob überprüfbare Gesamtreduktiongröße vorgegeben» (p. 10); the intertemporal guarantee is «eine abwehrrechtliche Grundrechtswirkung», not a Schutzpflicht (p. 9). It is the same Court that clarifies VW: whoever takes up the condition of the recognizable budget takes up this order. Civil courts against companies: LG München I 7/2/2023 and OLG München 12/10/2023 (DUH v. BMW, `deutsche-umwelthilfe-duh-v-bayerische-motoren-werke-ag-bmw-judgment_d48d` and `..._4b9f`) and LG Braunschweig 14/2/2023 (Kaiser v. Volkswagen, `kaiser-et-al-v-volkswagen-ag-judgment_88f8`): they cite paras. 122, 183, 192, 197-198 as the plaintiffs' arguments and distinguish them («das Bundesverfassungsgericht hat sich nicht mit der Frage einer privatrechtlichen Duldungspflicht beschäftigt, sondern mit dem Eingriffsbegriff», Kaiser, p. 16). Under rule 5.6, the plaintiffs' arguments count as a taking up (flagged), the courts' distinction does not. Engels v. Germany (ECtHR, dec. 1/7/2025, `engels-and-others-v-germany-decision_78c2`): describes Neubauer «summarised in Verein KlimaSeniorinnen» (§ 2) and the 2021 law at 65% (§ 3); inadmissible. Lliuya v. RWE (OLG Hamm, `luciano-lliuya-v-rwe-ag-order_5065`, p. 5) names the referral Beschluss. (VW, PI)

### 4.24 Formulae on future generations without legal construction

Brundtland 1987 and Rio 1992 (Principle 3), the public trust in Juliana, the heritage in T-411/1992: they state that posterity matters, without Vorwirkung nor proportionality (excluding `prov_brundtland`, `prov_rio_principio3`, `prov_juliana_public_trust`, `prov_T411_patrimonio`). A distinctive marker of Neubauer: «intertemporal», not «intergenerational». Texts by Brundtland and Rio [not verified in the corpus]. (VW, PI)

## 5. How to recognise a resumption

### 5.1 Strong indications
- Name, docket number, ECLI, BVerfGE 157, 30, «Klimabeschluss», or a form of `neubauer.yaml`; «German Federal Constitutional Court», «GFCC», «Bundesverfassungsgericht», «Karlsruhe» in a climate context with date 24/3/2021 or 29/4/2021.
- A Rn. or a Leitsatz of section 2 without a name: decipher them with 2.1 and 2.3.
- The formulas of section 3 or their faithful translations: «intertemporal guarantees of freedom», «advance interference-like effect», «offloaded onto the future», «radical abstinence», «one generation must not be allowed», «special duty of care» with «sufficiently reliable indications», «justiciable legal provision» with «election cycles» and «no voice of their own», «held to its own specification», «remaining national CO2 budget» as a parameter, «conceivable in principle» with «would not in any case have the same content», «solely objective» towards future generations.
- Two or more rings in the Neubauer order (irreversibility and budget, justiciable and concretized Art. 20a, Vorwirkung on the living, proportionality between generations, precautions and planning), even with other sources alongside.
- The two-outcome structure: duty of care existing but not violated, violation for future freedom.

### 5.2 False friends
KlimaSeniorinnen (Art. 8, «do its part», «overall remaining carbon budget» § 550, «disproportionate burden on future generations» § 549, reduced/wide margin); Urgenda (Arts. 2 and 8 ECHR, minimum set by the court, order of result); Colombia (holders of rights of future generations, Amazon as subject); OC-32/25 (healthy climate, «debida diligencia reforzada», mitigation target); Duarte and Sacchi (jurisdiction, effective control); ICJ and ITLOS (due diligence «stringent», NDCs, interstate obligations of conduct); Grande-Synthe and Ireland (statutory trajectory, without constitution); Shell (enterprise, UNGPs); Kalkar and BND (duty oriented to the future, binding abroad, in general); IPCC and SRU (budget figures); Paris and UNFCCC (threshold, CBDR); Brundtland, Rio, public trust (future generations without technique). Polarity: whoever states the rejected defense (future risk not current, future restrictions hypothetical, future generations as rights holders, uncertainties too great to conclude, Art. 20a not justiciable, no duty towards abroad) does not take up the proposition; whoever rejects it with the words of Neubauer does. The ratio of the BVerfG of 18/1/2022 (no Vorwirkung without a recognizable budget) and the judgments DUH v. BMW and Kaiser v. VW (no private-law duty arising from the Vorwirkung) are limits set by German courts, not taken up.

### 5.3 Uncertain cases
- Residual budget as a requirement in a text subsequent to 9/4/2024 without a name: if «overall remaining carbon budget», the five requirements, «do its part», 53600/20 appear, the source is KlimaSeniorinnen (ANOTHER_SOURCE with nesting on BC, see 5.4); if «remaining national CO2 budget that is derived from the remaining global budget», «albeit not precisely quantifiable», «special duty of care», «sufficiently reliable indications» appear, it is Neubauer.
- Future generations in a text subsequent to 29/5/2025: OC-32/25 deals with them on its own behalf but cites para. 146 for the objective duty; with «solely objective»/«exclusivamente objetiva» it is Neubauer, with an autonomous right to a healthy climate it is OC-32/25.
- «Intergenerational» alone catches Colombia, Rio, Brundtland: «intertemporal» is needed, or the pair generation and reduction burden with proportionality, or the discharge «offloaded onto the future».
- A text stating that the State cannot invoke the emissions of others: it is Urgenda nested via Neubauer if it cites Neubauer or Leitsatz 2c; it is Urgenda if it cites the Hoge Raad; it is KlimaSeniorinnen if «drop in the ocean» with § 444; it is international custom if no-harm and Trail Smelter.
- Before 24/3/2021 no one can take up the Beschluss: the appeals (Vollbremsung, per capita budget, the right to a dignified future), which are acts of the parties, can be taken up.

### 5.4 Mediated references
Rn. 203 and headnote 2c are expressly based on Hof 64 and HR 5.7.7 (with VG Berlin, BVerwG, Thomson, Ninth Circuit); Rn. 200 on Rechtbank 4.90; Rn. 157 on Rechtbank 4.75 and HR 7.5.2; Rn. 161 on HR 4.2, 4.4 and Ireland 3.7; Rn. 121 on HR 7.4.3; Rn. 218 on HR 2.1, 4.6, 7.4.3 and Ireland 4.3; Rn. 253 and 262 on Ireland 6.45 et seq. and 6.37 et seq. If the passage takes up the proposition from one of these points, the chain passes through the intermediate source: indicate Urgenda (or Ireland) in the «fondamento» and mark the reference as nested (codebook, dimension «annidata») instead of classifying it as OTHER_SOURCE of Neubauer. This is the case with the Brussels Court of Appeal § 160 and the Prague Municipal Court. In the opposite direction: KlimaSeniorinnen § 571 is based on Neubauer Rn. 215-229, Engels § 2 on KlimaSeniorinnen §§ 254 et seq., the Czech Supreme Court and OC-32/25 (note 27) on Neubauer for the reliability of the IPCC. Whoever takes up from these points takes up Neubauer in nested form, and this must be stated. If, instead, they take up from a point that is not based on Neubauer (KlimaSeniorinnen § 442 on CBDR, § 444 on the «drop in the ocean»; Duarte § 213 on jurisdiction), it is OTHER_SOURCE.

### 5.5 Who speaks
The name may appear in the descriptive part (KlimaSeniorinnen §§ 254-257, Duarte §§ 64-65, Engels §§ 1-3), in a party's submissions (Praga §§ 81-83, Czech Supreme Court [73], Sacchi § 7.3 and § 7.5, Brussels p. 146, DUH v. BMW and Kaiser v. VW in the plaintiffs' submissions, the briefs of Juliana, the ICJ and the ITLOS), in an intervention (KlimaSeniorinnen § 408), in a separate opinion (Eicke, pp. 238 and 249), in a table of authorities (La Rose, pp. 15 and 33). State it in the «mossa»: who asserts it, and whether the adjudicating body adopts it (KlimaSeniorinnen § 571, Brussels §§ 160, 201, 234, 248, Sacchi § 9.17, BVerfG 18/1/2022), summarises it, or distinguishes it (Brussels § 195, Kaiser v. VW p. 16, Praga on the budget).

### 5.6 The group's rules on what counts as a reuse (decided by the PI on 23/9/2026, applicable to all cases)
1. The same rationale applied to a private undertaking also counts: if a text states that the emissions permitted today to a company consume a budget and impose stricter restrictions on future generations, or that the undertaking cannot evade liability by pointing to the emissions of others, this is a reuse of the proposition even if the addressee is not the State.
2. It also counts if it is stated by a party, an intervener, or an amicus, not only by the court: the classification records that the proposition circulates, and the «mossa» indicates who advances it.
3. It also counts if the court summarises it as a party's argument: in that case, it must be flagged as such (who is speaking, and whether the court adopts it, distinguishes it, or does not decide on it).
